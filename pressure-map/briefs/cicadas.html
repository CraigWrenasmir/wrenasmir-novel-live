SCENE SCAFFOLD

Cicada Denial-of-Service

Rank: The Road Years Alive Tier: Fleck · 1 p Plan §10 Band: Root Comic counterweight to the storm

The job — what this scene must accomplish

One page of dry, physical comedy that lands directly after the storm's three pages of wonder and deflates the grandeur without undoing it. The loads:

  • The landscape overwhelms the network by sheer volume. Summer cicadas saturate the acoustic sensors: every channel pinned, storage filling with one enormous note. January simply out-transmits the archive, and the archive's only answer is to keep writing it down.
  • The nodes log the event faithfully. That fidelity is the joke and the tenderness at once — a machine dutifully archiving fourteen hours of scream because nobody told it screaming wasn't culture. The backstage aphorism — the bush has more bandwidth than the archive — is the writer's law; the page never says it, and no character thinks it.
  • Keep the humour dry and bodily. Heat, noise with a physical push, a maintenance job conducted in mime. Never whimsical, never cute, no twinkle. The comedy is the comedy of two competent people defeated by an insect, and taking it well.

Where it sits

Currently remainder p. 18 (≈ MS p. 70) — the last page of "The Road Years Alive," immediately after the storm (pp. 15–17). The adjacency is the design: the mesh that was one body in the rain is, three days or three weeks later, a set of overwhelmed microphones in the heat. The order can shift on the Pressure Map, but this fleck should travel with the storm — it is that scene's pressure-release valve.

Feeds directly into: the co-authorship band — organic integration (ants, resin, fungi) is this page's logic slowed down: the landscape getting into the archive, first by volume, later by meaning. Pays off from: the rooftop's cicadas "starting and stopping like a dropped connection" — the same insects, now on the other end of the line.

Dialogue permissions

Katita may

Shout single words at arm's length and give up on sentences entirely. Conduct her half of the maintenance job in gesture. Write one deadpan annotation in the log — one, and it must be dry as a stick, an entry a stranger could mistake for procedure. Line the shed cicada moults up along the dash.

Leif may

Attempt the sensor check anyway, mouthing the instructions. Do the storage arithmetic in his head — at this rate the node archives nothing but January until March — and decide, visibly, not to stop it. Know the insect by a different name than the last town used. Sit in the truck afterwards with his ears ringing, not talking, because talking is finally possible and not worth it.

No one may

Say bandwidth, signal, noise, attack, drowned out, overload — no engineering metaphor may reach for the event, because the event is the metaphor and doesn't know it. Nobody finds it meaningful, poignant, or symbolic. Nobody compares it to the storm. The insects get no adjectives of intent.

Sensory bank — raw material, take what serves

  • The wall of it at 40 degrees: sound with an actual push, a pressure on the sternum; walking into the tree line like walking into shallow surf.
  • The level meters pinned since 9:40 a.m. — a graph that is one flat line along the top of its own range, the shape of an instrument with nowhere left to go.
  • Conversation at a shout from half a metre, then by mouthing, then by hand — screwdriver mimed, cable traced in the air, thumbs up delivered like semaphore.
  • Moulted shells everywhere: on bark, on the guy-wire, a perfect split-backed one clinging to the node casing itself as if the archive had hatched something.
  • A live one on the antenna — green, immovable, singing the mast like it owns the infrastructure. Which, by volume, it does.
  • The folk taxonomy: Green Grocer, Yellow Monday, Black Prince, Double Drummer, Floury Baker — playground names, traded like currency; the same insect answering to different names one town back.
  • The all-stop: mid-afternoon the bush switches off all at once, one second to the next, and the silence has edges; a shouted word lands whole in it, enormous and domestic.
  • Heat props: bore-water taste, cordial gone warm, the vinyl seat branding the backs of legs, sweat in the eyes during the fine-motor part of the job.
  • Dusk: the graph steps down three decibels and no further; crickets take the channel without a handover ceremony.
  • In the cab after: ears ringing in two different keys, the ringing discussed with one look and dismissed.

Traps — the known failure modes

  • Whimsy. The instant the cicadas are charming, plucky, or conspiratorial, the page dies. They are a fact of January, as indifferent as the heat. Dry and physical, every line.
  • Importing the title. No character reaches for attack vocabulary, network metaphor, or the shorthand joke. The wit on the page must be smaller than the wit of the premise, or it reads as the author laughing first.
  • Meaning creep. One sentence tilting toward "the bush was trying to say —" and the fleck becomes a parable. The nodes log; the people sweat; nobody concludes.
  • Undoing the storm. The counterweight deflates the grandeur, not the truth. If the comedy mocks the mesh or the project, the storm's wonder is retroactively cheapened. The two pages must be able to be true at once.
  • Overstaying. One page. No second location, no evening scene, no reflection in the cab beyond the ringing ears. Flash and vanish is the tier and the punchline's survival.

Ten sparks — optional ways in, steal freely

  1. Open at the meter, not the noise: every acoustic channel pinned since 9:40 a.m., the graph a plateau at the ceiling of its range — January rendered as an instrument's helplessness before a single word about sound.
  2. The maintenance visit as full mime: an entire sensor check conducted in gesture and eyebrow, flawlessly, both of them discovering the size of the vocabulary they didn't know they'd built.
  3. A Double Drummer takes up residence on the antenna for the whole job and cannot be argued with. Leif relocates it twice with the gentleness of a man moving a sleeping child. It returns both times. The node records all three conversations.
  4. The all-stop lands mid-shout: the bush switches off between one syllable and the next and Katita's yelled "—LUNCH" arrives whole into perfect silence, the loudest civic announcement the valley has heard all week.
  5. Leif's storage arithmetic: at current volume the node will hold nothing but January until March. He watches the counter a moment, and lets it run. The decision made with a face, not a sentence.
  6. Katita's one log annotation, dry as procedure — "input: summer. no fault." — entered, capped, done. (If it reads as wit, dry it further or cut it; the log must stay the straight man.)
  7. The species argument settled on paper because speech is gone: he writes GREEN GROCER on the pad; the servo two towns back called the same insect a Yellow Monday; both names stand, unadjudicated, next to the torque figures.
  8. The moult on the casing: she frees it with one fingernail and stands it on the dash beside the others — a row of split-backed sentries facing the windscreen. One touch, no inventory of what it means.
  9. Playback weeks later as the closing beat: checking an unrelated file they get January at full gain through the truck speakers and both flinch like scalded cats — archived summer arriving with its push intact. One line of laughter, then the volume knob.
  10. End on scale, delivered flat: the node's whole job this month is to hold one note the bush is holding louder. Render that as image — the little LCD steady under the roar — and stop the page before anyone can be wise about it.

Exit condition

One page. The reader laughs somewhere in the middle of it — a dry laugh, the kind the heat permits — and turns the page with the storm's awe intact and newly proportioned: the mesh that was one body is also a thing an insect can out-shout, and both facts hold. Nothing is concluded, nothing is mourned, nobody is wise. If the last line explains the joke, cut the last line. The nodes are still logging as the page ends; that's the whole report.
