Borrowed Places

Rank: Aftermath (braided)Tier: Fleck · 1 pBand: PaperbackPlants the courtesan's ancestry

The job

One Paperback-band fleck: the couch-surfing summer, finally staged. Three loads: service the band (the slacker years have had no page since the book's opening movement); deliver the ASCII couch-map — the schematic of girlfriends' houses, couches, rooftops, the Cooks Hill Bookstore, beaches, drains — a map of temporary shelter and unstable social geography; and plant the courtesan's composite ancestry — these rooms, these girlfriends, these summers are what she will later be recognised as having been assembled from. Never stated.

Where it sits

Aftermath rank, directly before the Courtesan Recognition — so the reader walks into the recognition with the raw materials fresh: rooms, couches, the readerly intimacy of borrowed places. Chronology backstage: this is the reverie reaching back to the early years from the aftermath's distance, which is itself in character for the decade of research.

Canon & laws

Canon: MS pp. 2–3 (paperbacks, letters, couches, golf courses, rooftops); the ledger's borrowed-places transmission (the map of intimacy). The withheld-name and masthead laws don't touch this page. Guards: the courtesan may NOT appear or be prefigured by name — only the rooms and the girls, as themselves; the map must be proportional-font-safe (dash-built, the house style: identical rows, dot corners, no apostrophes); one page only.

Sparks

  • The map drawn as if from memory failing — a couch marked with a question mark, a house that might have been two houses.
  • Each couch remembered by its ceiling — what he saw falling asleep.
  • A paperback left behind in every house, titles listed like a lending record no one kept.
  • One girlfriend remembered only as a reading lamp's angle.
  • End on the map itself — the page's ASCII intrusion — with no comment after it.
