The Carriage Returns

Rank: Co-Authorship (braided)Tier: Fleck · 1 pBand: TrainPresent tense — the frame itself

The job

One page of pure train-present, directly after Bell-Birds. The reverie surfaces: the actual window, the actual light, a station passing, an announcement half-heard — then dives again. Three loads: give the reader air after the hinge (and protect Bell-Birds from bleeding into explanation); reassert the nested ontology (the carriage is the surface all the layers hang from, not another layer's memory); rehearse the book's final movement — withdrawal from system into train — in miniature, so the arrival lands as a homecoming, not a surprise.

Where it sits

Co-authorship rank, directly after Bell-Birds Convergence. Present tense throughout — this is the frame, not a flashback. Standing doctrine seated with this block: two-to-three-sentence train slivers are licensed at ANY block seam, costing no pages — the reverie surfacing for a breath between memories. This page is the doctrine at full length, once.

Canon & laws

The ostinato may be present as rhythm, never named. No reflection on what was just remembered — the carriage doesn't discuss the reverie; it simply IS. Fassifern and Hawkesbury River are the canonical pause-stations (p. 1). The tapping must not resolve here (the arrival owns the added beat). What the window shows is enough: pylons, water, the backs of towns. If the page starts meaning something, dim it until it's just a train again.

Sparks

  • Open on the announcement — garbled, half-heard, naming a station that's already passing.
  • The window as the book's oldest screen: the landscape streaming at its own bitrate.
  • Another passenger does something small and human (folds a jacket, shares a look) — the first stranger in nine pages.
  • The ostinato returns under everything, unnamed, one beat off from memory.
  • End mid-motion: the train neither arriving nor leaving, just carrying — and the next memory beginning without transition.
