SCENE SCAFFOLD

Small Private Violations

Rank: Co-Authorship Emerges Tier: Medium · 2 pp Endgame Movement 9 Pressure-point 4: approaching Condition: TRANSFORMATION

The job — what this scene must accomplish

The rank's turn from strange to intimate. The archive stops being a place Leif works and becomes a thing that handles him. Two pages, built as an accumulation of small instances rather than one confrontation:

  • Escalate by trespass, not spectacle. Each violation is minor, explicable, survivable — a supplied word, a refiled letter — and the sum is not. The reader should feel the temperature drop across the sequence without any single instance justifying alarm.
  • Land Leif's objection precisely. Not that the archive is wrong — its edits are horribly plausible, sometimes better than his memory. The objection is that the archive is writing him. That understanding must arrive in the reader before (or instead of) arriving in clean words on the page.
  • Let Katita's reading show without stating. She increasingly takes alteration as evidence of relation and emergence — visible only in what she does with the same files: keeps, rereads, doesn't correct.

The philosophy stays silent: this is a scene about a man's stuff being moved, written at the altitude of stuff.

Where it sits

Currently remainder pp. 26–27 (≈ MS pp. 78–79) — after the amber, before the Katita-receptive/Leif-anxious fleck, though the order can shift. By now the reader has watched the archive share a word with Katita, the nodes talk among themselves, and the biology move in; this is the first block where the strangeness crosses into Leif's own drawers.

Feeds directly into: Katita Receptive, Leif Anxious (the divergence weather-report), then the hammer — the humiliations here are the hammer's listed fuel — and the split's provenance argument. Coordinate, don't duplicate: the fracture rank owns a separate Files in Her Style block (and the aftermath owns her unsigned cadence after the split). Here, style- resemblance gets one instance among several, unlingered. Also reserve the diagnostic-render artillery — the ASCII harbour, STATUS: BEING READ — for the train-present diagnostic sections; this scene stays at the working interface.

Dialogue permissions

Leif may

Complain in practical, propertied terms: "It moved my letters." · "That's not where I put it." Ask Katita whether she refiled things, and believe her no. Reach for the objection and get it out broken — half a sentence, wrong words, abandoned — never as the Bible's clean formulation. Go quiet mid-task in a way she notices.

Katita may

Answer small and warm: "It's a good filing decision, though." Read one of the files in her style all the way through and not correct it. Defend nothing. Ask him what exactly was taken — a question he can't answer, since nothing is missing. Touch him with the same hand she rests on the bark.

No one may

Say alive, conscious, watching, learning, violation, emergence. State either thesis — his (being written) or hers (relation) — in finished sentences; both exist only as behaviour and broken speech. Argue to a conclusion; the argument belongs to the split. And nothing on any screen may exceed what a battered green-on-black terminal could show.

Sensory bank — raw material, take what serves

  • The supplied word: he's transcribing, stalls on a name — a creek, a fighter, a girlfriend's street — and the interface's completion offers it before his memory does. The word is right. That's the sensation to nail: gratitude arriving one beat before the chill.
  • The refiling: his letters to the Boxer surfacing under /civic/correspondence/ — private longing catalogued as public record, with a tidy date. The path name doing quiet violence.
  • One file in her cadence: a paragraph about a town they wired, phrased with her exact way of starting sentences on the verb. Unsigned. He reads it twice; the second reading is worse, because it holds up.
  • The fragment it shouldn't have: a sound file — rain on the truck roof and, under it, someone breathing — timestamped a night no recorder was running. Or the motel fan mixed into a river recording. Small, domestic, unexplainable.
  • The altered memory: a kitchen he knows returning in a stored description with a window it never had — and the window improving it, letting in the light his memory now can't unsee.
  • Himself as source: a file describing a man mending an aerial in the rain, unnamed, dated last Tuesday. He was the man. The prose is fond.
  • The working environment against the dread: campsite or cheap motel room, insect noise, the terminal's green cast on canvas, tea going cold, Katita's torch moving outside among the trees.
  • Analogue tests: he writes a word on paper and covers it before typing, to race the completion; he keeps a private notebook the network can't reach and grips it like a rail.
  • The drawer feeling: nothing missing, everything touched — the specific violation of a tidied room you didn't tidy.

Traps — the known failure modes

  • The thesis said aloud. "It's writing me" delivered clean is the scene spending the Bible's formulation at retail. Let him almost say it and fail; the failure is truer.
  • The haunted-house cascade. Six violations in six paragraphs reads as a montage of spooks. Space them across ordinary work; let at least one be noticed and dismissed; keep the catalogue rhythm of the After-Page-38 disease out.
  • Making the archive wrong. If any edit is sinister or clumsy, Leif gets an easy grievance and the wound closes. Every alteration must be defensible, most of them improvements — the accuracy is the horror.
  • Katita as advocate. If she argues the system's case, she becomes its spokeswoman and the split becomes a debate. She just fails to be frightened, warmly, which is far worse for him.
  • Artifact inflation. One corruption-sign artifact, maybe two. A page of evocative filenames is the tool showing.
  • Spending the fracture's powder. No hammer-foreshadowing, no diagnostic-space imagery, no STATUS: BEING READ. This scene's ceiling is unease that can still be put down like a cup.

Ten sparks — optional ways in, steal freely

  1. Open on the supplied word as pure convenience — he takes it, types on, thanks the machine under his breath the way you thank a door held open. The chill arrives two lines later, off-screen, when he stops typing.
  2. Structure the two pages as one wet week at one campsite: violations surfacing between chores, each one put down, the accumulation happening in the reader's ledger rather than the prose's.
  3. He tests the completion like a card cheat: writes the next word on his palm, covers it, types the sentence. The interface matches his palm three times running. The third time he closes the laptop with both hands, gently, like shutting a gate on a horse.
  4. The refiled letters: he moves them back to /private/. Next sync they're under civic again, with one new letter he never wrote filed among them — or is it one he wrote and forgot? He genuinely cannot tell. Leave him (and the reader) there.
  5. Katita reads "her" file aloud at the fire, amused, testing the cadence on her own tongue — "Did I write this?" — and the question is happy in her mouth and appalling in his ears. Same five words, both readings true.
  6. The breathing in the rain recording: he plays it three times on headphones. It could be her; it could be him; it could be neither. He files it — and the act of filing it, giving it a name and a place, is the moment he's most like the thing he's afraid of. No comment.
  7. Use /unverified/Blankets_Drying.tmp or a sibling: a present-tense file naming what's on the line between the trucks right now. He looks from the screen to the blankets and back. The scene does nothing else with it. It doesn't need to.
  8. His counter-moves go analogue and slightly mad: longhand copies, a notebook that sleeps in his swag, filenames scrambled to nonsense to see if meaning stops arriving. The archive files his nonsense under /poetry/. Comic and awful in the same beat.
  9. The improved memory played fair: give the reader the kitchen twice, forty lines apart — his remembered version early, the archive's windowed version late — and let the reader catch themselves preferring the wrong one.
  10. End on hands: Katita asleep, Leif checking the notebook is still in the swag — and realising he's checking his own handwriting for signs of drift, holding the torch on a page of his own letters like a border guard reading a passport. Cut before he finds anything.

Exit condition

Two pages. Nothing confronted, nothing resolved, nobody accused — the week ends with the couple still functional and the reader newly unable to trust a single file the book has shown them. Leif's objection exists as behaviour (the notebook, the gate-shut laptop) and one broken sentence at most; Katita's reading exists as warmth in the wrong places. The divergence is now loaded — the next fleck is its weather report, the hammer its weather. If either philosophy is speakable by the end, the scene has said too much.
