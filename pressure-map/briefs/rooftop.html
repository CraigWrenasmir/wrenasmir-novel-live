SCENE SCAFFOLD

The Rooftop as a Real Party

Rank: Origins & Pleasure Tier: Medium · 2 pp Endgame Movement 5 Pressure-point 1: the wound / spark Condition: INSTINCT

The job — what this scene must accomplish

This is where ROOT is born, and it must be born sideways — as the strange consequence of one local truth being made too smooth, at a party, between two people flirting. Three loads, carried lightly:

  • Katita enters as presence, not thesis. Body, costume, wit, attention. The reader must want her in the book before the book uses her.
  • The Elias Shore wound sparks. Not as philosophy — as a young man's over-invested party monologue that one listener recognises as having structure.
  • The vaporwave avatar gets its human origin. Her naming of his accidental costume — "What are you, a bushranger from the future?" — is the seed the diagnostic render grows from, decades later. It must land as a joke, not an omen.

Ratified guardrail: a real social and sensory world before it becomes philosophical. If a page must be cut, cut philosophy, keep party.

Where it sits

Currently remainder pp. 1–2 (≈ MS pp. 53–54) — the reader has just left the diagnostic density of pp. 38–52. This scene is the book exhaling: heat, bodies, laughter after machine-space. The contrast is the point; let the first paragraph be almost pure weather and noise.

Feeds directly into: Primes on Skin / the Biro (p. 3 — their first private protocol can begin the same night or soon after), and everything ROOT. Pays off from: the shed and Fighters of the North already seeded in the manuscript's suburban backyard; the reader may half-remember the name Shore.

Dialogue permissions

Katita may

Name his costume before he can. Ask the five structural questions. Tease him about his intensity and mean it as a compliment. Reframe signal as a graph problem. Deliver the routing line as a solution to a technical problem he raised — never as wisdom. Draw.

Leif may

Over-tell the Shore story with too many specifics (fight dates, the star on the shorts, the misprinted names). Say "the internet gets him wrong" and mean it as loyalty. Be embarrassed at his own heat. Notice her skirt is a coordinate system before he notices anything else.

No one may

Say archive, preservation, memory as themes. Say anything resembling "systems flatten local truth." Predict ROOT, name a project, or plan anything. Understand the night as important. The scene's future weight must be invisible to everyone inside it.

Sensory bank — raw material, take what serves

  • Gravel-and-bitumen roof still radiating the day's heat up through thongs; the tin awning below ticking as it cools.
  • An esky of melted ice, cans standing in the slush like buoys; goon cask on a string tied to the TV aerial (spin-the-cask abandoned two hours ago).
  • Fairy lights run from a bedroom window on an extension lead that also feeds the amp — when the bass hits, the lights dim in rhythm: the party's own load-shedding.
  • Harbour: gantry cranes lit like standing herons; the channel markers pulsing; coal ships at anchor offshore, evenly spaced, patient — watch batteries in their packaging.
  • A mozzie coil in a jar lid; someone's costume shedding sequins into it.
  • Smells: sunscreen not yet worn off, cask wine, hot galvanised iron, cut grass from the yard below, someone frying halloumi in the kitchen two floors down.
  • Sounds: ambient electronica off a laptop, argument about the playlist, a train braking somewhere down the line (one free ostinato echo — don't underline it), cicadas starting and stopping like a dropped connection.
  • Costumes glimpsed once each: a nun with a stubby holder, two Ned Kellys who avoid each other all night, a bedsheet ghost texting through the eyeholes, someone as "the internet" in a shirt of cables.
  • Stairwell physics: the queue for the bathroom as a slow data packet moving through the house; conversations relayed up the stairs, arriving slightly wrong.
  • A gaff-taped costume repair mid-party — tape holding a body together so it can keep working (quiet rhyme with taped hands, taped paperbacks; one image, no comment).

Traps — the known failure modes

  • The lecture. If Leif's Shore talk runs more than half a page unbroken, the party has died. Let the party interrupt him at the worst moment (costume-contest cheer, blown fuse) — the obsession is proven alive by surviving interruption.
  • Summary-time drift. "That night they talked for hours" is the pp. 38–52 disease. Stay inside minutes, not hours; end the scene before the party ends.
  • Katita as concept. If she only asks smart questions, she's a device. Give her a body: how she sits on the parapet, what she does with her cup, the pen behind her ear where the constellation parting begins.
  • Solving Shore. Neither of them may reach a conclusion about what the contradiction means. Instinct only. The bedroom scene owns the concept; the bushwalk owns relation.
  • Omen-lighting the plants. The grevillea, the tape, the train braking — one touch each, zero commentary. If a motif knows it's a motif, cut it.

Ten sparks — optional ways in, steal freely

  1. Open mid-costume-contest, judged by strangers on the next roof shouting scores across the gap — the first public organ of the night is two rooftops voting.
  2. Katita counts the coal ships aloud, calls them "a battery bank for a city that hasn't decided what it's for," then looks embarrassed at her own aphorism — she distrusts her aphorisms; it's why she'll trust engineering.
  3. Leif's jetpack is two goon bladders sprayed silver. All night people ask if there's wine left in them. There is.
  4. Her pen dies mid-diagram; she pulls the pen from his beard without asking. The theft is the first intimacy. The napkin diagram is in his pen from the start.
  5. The Ned Kellys: Leif watches the two identical outlaws studiously ignore each other and thinks of Shore's names refusing to be the same man. One image; he doesn't articulate it.
  6. Someone's phone dies mid-argument-settling ("just Google it") right as Leif starts on Shore — so for one conversation, the smooth answer is unavailable and the local account is the only source on the roof.
  7. She asks the five questions across the whole scene, not in a row — one per return, between other conversations, coming back to him like a node retrying.
  8. The routing line arrives while she's drawing paths around a wine stain on the napkin — the stain is a dead node; she routes the message around the damage that's already on the table.
  9. Heat lightning far up the coast, too far for thunder: the sky exchanging packets silently. Leif notices; says nothing; this is what noticing is for.
  10. End on skin, not summary: the napkin folded into his shirt pocket against his chest; or the grevillea transferred, lopsided, to her hair; the ink of the diagram not yet dry, printing a faint wrong-way-round topology onto the cotton over his heart.

Exit condition

Two pages. The scene ends while the party is still going — mid-noise, on a body-detail, with nothing decided. The reader should close the scene thinking they've just watched two people meet at a good party, and only later realise they watched a network being invented, a wound being named, and an avatar being born. If it feels important while it's happening, it isn't finished.
