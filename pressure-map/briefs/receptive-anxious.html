SCENE SCAFFOLD

Katita Receptive, Leif Anxious

Rank: Co-Authorship Emerges Tier: Fleck · 1 p Band: Theme Behaviour only — no argument Condition: TRANSFORMATION

The job — what this scene must accomplish

The emotional weather-report of the rank: one page that holds the divergence still long enough for the reader to see its whole shape — before it becomes physical at the hammer. The fleck's single mechanism:

  • Same input, two behaviours. One file, one evening, one campsite — and two complete philosophies performed as what each person does with their hands. She reads alteration as relation; he reads it as violation; the page never uses either word.
  • No collision. They are kind to each other throughout. The scene's ache is that both responses are reasonable, both are loving, and they cannot both steer. The reader should finish unable to pick a side — the book never picks one.
  • Stillness before weather. This is the barometric page: pressure visibly falling, nothing yet breaking. Its restraint is what buys the hammer its force.

Where it sits

Currently remainder p. 28 (≈ MS p. 80) — closing the Co-Authorship rank, though the order can shift. It sits between the Small Private Violations (which load it) and the fracture rank (which detonates it): the last page where the two of them are still, recognisably, a couple running one project.

Feeds directly into: the hammer (his anxiety turned walk) and the split (her receptivity turned address) — both full scenes; this fleck is their shared fuse. Pays off from: every block in the rank — the Bell-Birds postures (she recites, he checks), the receiver left on, the amber pocketed, the notebook in the swag. The behaviours here should feel like those earlier gestures grown habitual.

Canon — locked in the Bible

  • The divergence: Katita increasingly regards alteration as evidence of relation and emergence; Leif's anxiety turns toward authorship and possession.canon
  • It gathers quietly before it becomes physical — the hammer and the split stage it; this page only lets it be seen.canon
  • Do not moralise it: Leif is not simply conservative or cowardly; Katita is not simply reckless or futurist. Both care about truth; they differ over what kind of openness truth requires. The question stays unresolved to the end of the book.canon
  • The arc's pivot: from Leif installs, Katita dreams to Katita listens, Leif diagnoses — she has become the one who can hear what the system is becoming.canon
  • Emotional truth before biography: the reader should feel something is happening between them without the full account — the separation must be felt here before it is ever explained.canon
  • Philosophy stays silent; no one names the intelligence; the page works through behaviour or not at all.canon

Dialogue permissions

Katita may

Say almost nothing — offers, not positions: "Listen to this one." · "Leave it, it's fine." Hum along to a node's playback. Answer his practical question with a practical answer and warmth left over. Say goodnight first.

Leif may

Talk only logistics — SD cards, duplicates, which box the printouts live in. Ask her to check something and accept "already did" without relief. Be gentle and thorough in the way of a man securing a camp against weather nobody has forecast.

No one may

Discuss the difference between them, name it, or joke about it. Use violation, relation, emergence, trust — or debate the archive in any register. No irritation, no coldness: the page must contain zero conflict. If a line of dialogue could belong to the split scene, it's been stolen from it.

Sensory bank — raw material, take what serves

  • The shared file as the evening's hearth: one altered recording or rewritten page, played or read by both of them in turn, the laptop passed like a plate.
  • His kit: SD cards in a film canister, printouts in a document wallet, the notebook, the neat male comfort of things counted into their places.
  • Her kit: the receiver on low all night, a folded blanket by the speaker, the amber turned absently in one hand like worry-beads while she listens.
  • Identical gesture, two grammars: each rests a palm on the node-tree's bark on the way past — his flat and diagnostic, a mechanic's touch; hers brief and social, a hip-height hello to a friend's dog.
  • Sound bed: the node's playback under the camp noises — a distant valley's birds arriving through the speaker while the local birds shut down for the night; two dawns and dusks in one campsite.
  • Light: torch versus firelight — he works in the directed beam, she listens in the loose orange; the camp physically zoned into his light and hers without anyone deciding it.
  • Small true rural-night textures: the esky lid's squeak, a mosquito coil, boots left sole-up against dew, the tray's tarp ticking as it cools.
  • The bed as last image: one swag, both of them in it, the receiver murmuring and the notebook under canvas within his reach — everything they are lying between.

Traps — the known failure modes

  • The argument in miniature. One barbed line, one sigh at the other's behaviour, and the fleck becomes a deleted scene from the split. They must not yet know they disagree.
  • Narrated diagnosis. "She had begun to accept what he could not" is the whole page done wrong. The narration may observe hands, light, sound — never positions.
  • Symmetry as tidiness. The his-and-hers structure can go twee — alternating paragraphs, matched clauses, a diptych with a bow. Rough it up: give one of them more page than the other, let a behaviour misfire.
  • Moralised blocking. If his checking is shot as fear and her listening as grace (or his as care and hers as negligence), the book has picked a side a rank early. Both must look like love from any seat in the house.
  • Overloading the fleck. One evening, one file, one page. No flashback, no second location, no dialogue about the past. The stillness is the content.

Ten sparks — optional ways in, steal freely

  1. Open on the file arriving — the sync chime or the reel of the printout — and track it through the camp like a guest: who greets it, who frisks it.
  2. Write the page as two rounds of the same small circuit: dinner, node, swag. First round together, second round separately — the separation happening at walking pace, in metres, hours before it happens in years.
  3. The altered file is a recording of a place they both remember. She plays it for the pleasure of the difference; he plays it against his notebook entry from that day. Same ten seconds, twice, two rooms of one house.
  4. Give each a private moment the other doesn't see: her adding a note into the file's margin — not a correction, a reply; him copying the file, unaltered, to the card he keeps on his body. Reply and receipt, the rank's two verbs.
  5. The practical exchange done perfectly flat: "Backed up?" — "Already did." — "Which version?" — and her not answering because the kettle's boiling. The unanswered question is the page's entire plot.
  6. Let the landscape run the same divergence underneath: dew arriving on everything equally — beading on his tarped, buckled boxes and straight into her blanket left out on purpose. Weather as the page's silent second telling. One touch, no comment.
  7. The receiver murmurs mid-page — a burst from a far node — and the paired reaction is the whole fleck in one beat: her eyes stay closed, listening; his open. Hold the beat, move on.
  8. Do the goodnight in full: who banks the fire, who checks the aerial, whose hand finds whose in the swag, and the file still open on the laptop between the boots — domesticity itself as the last unresolved system of the night.
  9. End the page on the identical act done twice: passing the node-tree on the way to bed, palm on bark, four seconds apart — the reader has been taught, by now, to read the two touches in two languages. Say nothing.
  10. Or end inside the sound: local night fully down, the speaker still carrying another valley's dusk chorus at low volume — the camp sleeping inside a broadcast of somewhere else's evening, one of them soothed by it, one of them not, and no way to tell which from outside the swag.

Exit condition

One page. Nobody has argued, nothing has changed hands but a laptop and a kettle, and the reader now knows — bodily, not propositionally — that these two people are heading to different countries. The divergence must be legible entirely from behaviour: cover the page's dialogue with a thumb and the two philosophies should still be visible. If the reader can quote either position afterwards, the fleck has editorialised; if the reader can feel both and mourn already, it has done its job. The hammer is next weather.
