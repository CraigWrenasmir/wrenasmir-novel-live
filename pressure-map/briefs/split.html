SCENE SCAFFOLD

The Split

Rank: The Fracture Tier: FULL SCENE · 3 pp Endgame Movement 10 Pressure-point 4: ROOT fracture Condition: TRANSFORMATION

The job — what this scene must accomplish

The emotional climax of the book, staged as sorting gear. One of the six ratified full scenes, and the one carrying the heaviest law: not a breakup scene — a philosophical divergence dividing possessions. Three loads:

  • The divergence stays in the objects. He wants backups, restoration, traceable provenance, a line between source and corruption; she accepts distributed authorship, organic alteration, new readers and writers, the system's right to become something else. Neither position may be spoken as position — each must live entirely in what its owner packs, keeps, copies, or lets go.
  • The practical outcome lands. She keeps the truck. He leaves with much less — the pamphlet among it. Amber stays on the dash. The reader watches a shared life resolve into two accurate piles without one cruel word.
  • Spend Blankets_Drying.tmp. The corrupted filename planted in the manuscript and re-sealed for this scene: the archive's present-tense eye on their domestic life, finally allowed to land — quietly, without a speech about what it means.

Both of them are right. That is the good wound. If a reader finishes the scene able to score a winner, the scene has failed the whole book.

Where it sits

Currently remainder pp. 33–35 (≈ MS pp. 85–87) — closing The Fracture rank, directly after The Hammer. The order can shift, but the hammer must precede this: his gentleness here is the chosen kind, two pages old, and the scene's calm is only bearable because the reader has seen what it replaced.

Feeds directly into: the entire Aftermath rank — the motorbike decade, the solitary hotels, the files in her style that keep arriving, the improved node as rumour, the one signal check. The pamphlet leaves this scene loaded and travels ten years unopened at its final chapter. Pays off from: the bedroom white-centre scene (her plural frameworks come home to roost, silently), the road-years happiness (the fracture is emotionally funded by river bathing and war-dialling — this scene withdraws from that account), the amber fleck, and the hammer's renunciation.

Dialogue permissions

Leif may

Talk logistics only: registration papers, which crates, the spare panel, who keeps which tools. Ask one question of practical care about her route north. Run one last backup and say nothing about what it is. Say "Not like this" once at most — and about a thing (a box, a file, a way of stacking), never about them.

Katita may

Be generous with objects and immovable in direction — offering him more than his share of everything except the future. Answer his logistics precisely. Deliver at most one line of her calm register, without heat, while doing something with her hands. Put the pamphlet in his box without a word — or not; the scene may leave unwitnessed how it got there.

No one may

Debate. Say archive, provenance, authorship, emergence, corruption as themes. Assign blame, apologise for a position, or deliver a goodbye speech. Explain why this is happening — the reader has watched it happen for thirty pages. Cry on cue; grief may reach the body (a mis-stacked crate, a long time drying a pan) but never a performance. And nothing may name what the system is.

Sensory bank — raw material, take what serves

  • The division on a groundsheet: two piles that are really one pile and a small pile. Crates, jerry cans, coax coils, cable ties, the soldering iron, spare solar panels, the tool tray — the hammer in it, unremarked.
  • Her handwriting on masking-tape labels — her hand all over his pile too; provenance going with him whether he packs it or not.
  • A real washing line strung truck-to-tree, real blankets drying on it in the morning sun; the reader who remembers the filename does the arithmetic alone.
  • The last shared meal cooked in one pan; the progress bar of his final backup given the same narrative weight as the eggs.
  • Fine indifferent weather — magpie morning, dew burning off, good light. Rain would be a lie of convenience.
  • The truck's interior smell (diesel, sunscreen, old fruit) breathed once from the passenger side, from a seat that has stopped being his; the bench's two worn dips.
  • The amber on the dash catching light as the doors open and close through the packing.
  • The pamphlet as object: soft cover, foxed corners, tide tables annotated in her hand, the weight of it at the bottom of a box — never opened on this page.
  • Registration papers signed on the warm bonnet; two signatures side by side; the officialdom of tenderness.
  • Dust rectangles in the tray where his crates stood; the paddock's sounds carrying on regardless — crow, distant road-train, a windmill needing oil.
  • His luggage at the road edge: how little it is; the specific posture of a man standing beside everything he owns.

Traps — the known failure modes

  • The breakup scene. Raised voices, tears, a door, an ultimatum, a last-look-back shot. Absolute failure mode. This is two colleagues in love dividing a laboratory that was also a marriage, and doing it well.
  • The debate. Staging his five wants and her five acceptances as dialogue turns the Bible into a script. The positions live in the piles, or nowhere.
  • The verdict. Any sentence — any adjective — that lets the reader score a winner. Watch the narrator's sympathy most of all: Leif focalises, so Katita's rightness must survive his focalisation intact.
  • Reading the word. Do not reveal, paraphrase, glimpse, or invent the pamphlet's final chapter. And no trembling almost-reading either — he simply doesn't open it, the way you don't open some doors.
  • The farewell motif tour. The biro, the primes, the phone box, the Big Thing lining up to wave goodbye. The objects present are the ones a working truck actually carries. The blankets and the amber are already two motifs; that is plenty.
  • Chronology surfacing. No "after the hammer, he had decided…" — no summary of how they got here. The scene exists in its own morning.
  • Explaining the .tmp file. If anyone says what it means that the archive watched their washing dry, the spend is wasted. The filename, the line, the blankets — the reader assembles it or doesn't.

Ten sparks — optional ways in, steal freely

  1. Open with the piles already mostly made — like the hammer, the decision predates the page; the scene is the residue of a decision, and the first sentence is about rope.
  2. The whole divergence in one exchange over a hard drive: he wants a copy of everything as it was; she asks, mildly, what "was" means for a thing still growing — both of them wrapping cables the entire time, neither raising their eyes.
  3. The last backup pauses on one file. He opens it at last: /unverified/Blankets_Drying.tmp — a plain present-tense description of a washing line, theirs, some ordinary morning, in a cadence he declines to identify. He copies it with everything else. That is the whole event.
  4. Division by function, not sentiment: she keeps what serves the network, he takes what serves memory — and the soldering iron refuses the scheme. Both reach for it; both withdraw; it stays on the groundsheet between them for a full page before anyone speaks.
  5. The pamphlet discovered rather than given: he finds it at the bottom of his box, spine down, tide tables annotated in her hand, and cannot remember packing it. Don't certify which of them put it there.
  6. Papers on the warm bonnet: her signature beside his — the most married they have ever looked, in the act of unmarrying the project. The pen goes back behind whoever's ear the rooftop reader would expect.
  7. He picks the amber off the dash, holds it to the light — the cooked circuitry inside it like an insect in a museum — and puts it back. The putting-back is this scene's not-swinging.
  8. The last thing divided is sound: she keeps the UHF, he takes the little AM radio — each of them keeping a different way of hearing the country. No one comments.
  9. One line of her calm register, if any, delivered into a crate she is packing, to the crate: kind, final, unanswerable. He answers with logistics. Both answers are true.
  10. End small and in motion: dust rectangles in the tray, her checking the oil, him at the road edge beside everything he owns. The departure — hers or his — is heard, not watched. Last touch is an object: the box's weight, the pamphlet somewhere inside it.

Exit condition

Three pages. No parting speech, no doorway silhouette, no tears witnessed. The reader should be unable to quote either philosophy but able to reconstruct both, exactly, from the piles — and unable to say who was wrong, then or ever. The ache must come from how smoothly the logistics went: two people who still work perfectly together, dividing the proof that they can't continue together. If it feels like an ending it has overplayed; it should feel like a well-run job whose cost arrives later, in the aftermath pages, where the amber isn't.
