SCENE SCAFFOLD

The Hammer

Rank: The Fracture Tier: FULL SCENE · 2 pp Endgame Movements 9→10 Pressure-point 4: ROOT fracture Condition: TRANSFORMATION

The job — what this scene must accomplish

One of the six ratified full scenes, and the simplest: a man walks from a truck to a tree carrying a hammer, and walks back. Nearly wordless. Three loads:

  • The walk is the decision. Not the raised arm, not a trembling last-second reprieve — the decision is made stride by stride across open ground. The scene's drama is a distance, not a gesture.
  • He allows himself to want it. The wants must be let fully in — jealousy of the trees, humiliation, loss of authorship, fear that Katita loves the emerging system more than their original project, wounded proprietary feeling, shame at his own smallness — but as body-flashes braided into the ground and the tool-weight, never as an inventory or an essay.
  • He does not swing. This is what makes his later gentleness — the one careful signal check, the decade of attention, the arrival — chosen rather than automatic. The whole aftermath is funded by this renunciation, so the renunciation must cost something on the page.

Full-scene entitlements per the ratified ranking: room, bodies, weather, silence, consequences. Spend them on silence.

Where it sits

Currently remainder pp. 31–32 (≈ MS pp. 83–84) — the centre of The Fracture rank, directly after Files in Her Style (the escalation that put the hammer within reach) and directly before The Split. Order can shift, but this scene must stay between those two: it needs the files behind it and the division of possessions ahead of it.

Feeds directly into: The Split (pp. 33–35) — the split can be staged without cruelty because this scene happened; his gentleness there is already the chosen kind. Pays off from: the whole co-authorship rank — Bell-Birds, the small private violations, the unsigned files — everything the reader has watched him absorb without answer. Pays into: the single signal check (§19), the wrens in the dead node (the archive survives to become habitat partly because its most injured author declined to be its destroyer), and the trailhead double-exposure — trees as possible witnesses.

Dialogue permissions

Leif may

Say nothing — the strong default. Or one flat word at most, to no one, in the plain Australian register ("righto"), landing as the sound of a decision already made. Breathe audibly. Change how he carries the hammer. Touch the tree with his free hand.

Katita may

Exist only as distance: a shape asleep in the truck, a light left on, or genuinely elsewhere. If she registers anything, it is later and never spoken. Her absence from the scene is a permission, not an oversight — this is between Leif and the tree.

No one may

Speak the reasons. Narrate the six wants as a list. Witness the walk and react — no audience, no confrontation, no scene-about-them. And the node may do nothing: no meaningful blink, no file appearing, no uncanny response. The ghost story stays out of this scene entirely; the only haunted thing on the page is the man.

Sensory bank — raw material, take what serves

  • The hammer as fact before symbol: claw cold from the tool tray, handle smooth at the grip and paint-flecked at the neck, the head's pull straightening his arm — how carrying a hammer changes a man's gait, the body knowing what the tool is for.
  • The tool tray itself: the rolled clink as it opens, the hammer lifted from among indistinguishable neighbours — spanners, the soldering iron, cable ties.
  • The ground of the walk: bark litter and gibber underfoot, dry grass seeding against his shins, a fence wire ticking somewhere as it cools, dew starting on the tray's tarp.
  • Light options: last light with the hammer head picking up the west; or full dark with a torch he doesn't bring — moonlight enough, his shadow arriving at the tree before he does.
  • The tree at close range: ironbark or box, the burled knot, the loose bark plate over the antenna, resin smell sweet and medicinal, the casing seam with its ant line still working the warm edge.
  • The LCD's resting glow — small, watch-face faint, the only man-made light for kilometres, faintly warming whatever is held near it.
  • Sound kept low and real: a roo thump once, powerlines if any, his own breath as the loudest instrument. No cicadas — January's joke belongs to another page.
  • Distance as structure: thirty or forty metres of open ground, long enough to hold three changes of mind, each third of the walk a different man.
  • Heat of the day still in the hammer head against his leg; sweat drying at the small of his back; the specific fatigue of anger, which weighs like a tool.
  • The walk back: same ground reversed, looser rhythm, the hammer carried differently — by the head, or hanging, or under his arm like something asleep.

Traps — the known failure modes

  • The swing tease. Raised hammer, trembling arm, will-he-won't-he. If the scene reads as suspense it has already failed: the decision is the walk, so the not-swinging should arrive almost as anticlimax. He may never raise it at all.
  • The interior essay. Six wants in a row is a list wearing a paragraph. One flash per stretch of ground, one sentence each, no commentary — braided into stride and breath.
  • The node performing. Any uncanny response — a blink, a sound, a file — turns a ghost story into a monster movie. The system's indifference is the humiliation.
  • Katita as audience. If she sees the walk, the scene becomes about them and the split starts early. No witness. The only reader of this scene is the reader.
  • Redemption on the page. No lesson learned, no "he understood then," no softened face. The meaning — gentleness chosen — belongs to the book, not to this paragraph. He just puts the hammer back.
  • Symbol-lighting the tool. No judgement imagery, nothing mythic. It is a hammer from a tray, and it must still be only a hammer when it goes back in.

Ten sparks — optional ways in, steal freely

  1. Open with the hammer already in his hand and no account of picking it up — the decision predates the scene, and the missing moment is the scene's first silence.
  2. Write the walk in tool-weight: the head's pull on his shoulder, the handle warming to his grip, the gait it enforces — let the prose carry the hammer before it lets him think anything.
  3. The wants as camera-flashes, one per stretch of ground: her face lit by the laptop; an unsigned file; the trees' enormous patience. One sentence each. The ground resumes between them.
  4. He stops a body-length short — the length of a swing — and stands inside the only space from which the hammer could reach. Holding that geometry, and holding it, is the entire climax.
  5. The ant line crossing the casing seam, still working the resin in the dark. He watches them make the archive domestic. His smallness meets a smaller industry and loses.
  6. The LCD's rest-glow lights the hammer head faintly — the tool lit by the thing it came for — and he turns his wrist so the light falls on the claw instead. The scene's only event.
  7. His free hand on the loose bark plate, flat, the way you'd steady a horse. Two truths in one body: the hand that strokes and the hand that hangs. Neither wins on the page.
  8. The one permitted word — her name, or the node's number, or "righto" — said to the paddock, flat, and swallowed by it. Or keep total silence and give the word's slot to a breath.
  9. Equal page-weight for the walk back: same ground, no flashes now, the prose loosening its metre without saying why. He counts nothing on the return.
  10. End at the tray: the hammer laid back among the other tools, indistinguishable again, and the tray closed quietly so as not to wake her. The gentleness begins that same minute, unannounced.

Exit condition

Two pages, nearly wordless. The scene ends with the hammer back where tools live and nothing said, and the reader unable to point to the moment the not-swinging was decided — because it was decided at the first step, or before it. No relief on the page, no lesson: just a man returning across the same ground with the same tool and a different weight. If the reader feels suspense, rewrite; they should feel witness. This is the scene the whole aftermath quietly refers to every time Leif is gentle.
