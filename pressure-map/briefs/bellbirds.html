SCENE SCAFFOLD

Bell-Birds Convergence

Rank: Co-Authorship Emerges Tier: Full scene · 3 pp Endgame Movement 9 One of the six ratified full scenes Condition: TRANSFORMATION

The job — what this scene must accomplish

This is the hinge of the whole co-authorship rank — the plan calls it one of the central turning points, and it is the atlas's top bet. Human memory and tree-memory are shown to have converged, and the scene must never say so. Three loads, carried in near-silence:

  • Provenance collapses and stays collapsed. Katita's misremembered word and the archive's stored word agree, and no test either of them can run will establish which yielded to which — or whether either did. The reader must leave unable to close the case, permanently.
  • The divergence gets its first true reading. Leif goes hunting for the canonical text; Katita recites on. Same event, two nervous systems. This is the split in embryo — shown as two sets of hands doing different things on the same night.
  • The corruption is revealed as intimate, not technical. Not a glitch in a file — a shared word between a woman and a network. The dread arrives through tenderness.

Ratified guardrail: mostly silent and physical. The hinge must not explain itself. If a paragraph starts adjudicating causality, cut it and put a body in its place.

Long-wire guard (2026-07-24): ROOT's very first file was a bellbird midi extracted from the student's sim (MS p. 44) — the founding and the corruption share a species, and the founding quietly seeds why Katita knows Kendall's poem. This scene must NEVER call back to the midi. No "the same bird," no container remembered, no narrator joining dots. The wire deepens possibility 4 (the change may have existed longer than either knows) only in the reader who joins it unassisted.

Where it sits

Currently remainder pp. 19–21 (≈ MS pp. 71–73) — opening the Co-Authorship rank, though the order can shift. The reader arrives from the road years: the storm has already made the network feel like one body, the cicadas have already out-shouted it, and poetry has been passing casually between Leif and Katita for pages. Her recitation should feel like a habit, not an event — that's what the road rank bought.

Feeds directly into: Nodes Conversing Alone (pp. 22–23 — after the archive shares a word with Katita, the modules sharing words with each other is the natural escalation), Small Private Violations, and ultimately the hammer and the split. Pays off from: the storm (the mesh as one body), the vernacular republic (memorised verse as a public organ), and the whole ecology of unstable provenance — Shore's plural names, the contaminated masthead. The altered poem joins that ecology; it does not found it.

Canon — locked in the Bible

  • The event: Katita recites Bell-Birds from memory and gets one word wrong. Leif checks the ROOT file, expecting the stored text to correct her. The file contains the same altered wording.canon
  • Causality cannot be established — four ways: she may have written her remembered version into the archive; the archive may have altered her memory; both may be responding to a third distributed process; the change may have existed longer than either knows. None is confirmed, ever.canon
  • Their responses: Leif searches for proof of the canonical text. Katita is less disturbed by the collapse of provenance.canon
  • Register: mostly silent and physical. One of the six ratified full scenes — room, bodies, weather, silence, consequences.canon
  • The corruption is meaning-production, not data loss — misrecognition, analogy becoming operational. What scares Leif is not that the trees interfere but that their edits feel accurate.canon
  • The intelligence is botanical, analogue, older than software — never named. No terminology may surface. The event must feel like weather, memory, love — not computation.canon
  • The altered poem joins the ecology of unstable provenance — alongside Elias Shore's names, the Boxer correspondence, the contaminated masthead, and (later) files in Katita's cadence. It is one instance, not the key.canon
  • The Bible already holds one Bell-Birds drift elsewhere: "It lives in the mountain" → "It leaves in the mountain" is logged as a corruption sign in the archive (Movement 9), with the Leif / leaf / life associations. Whether that is the word Katita gets wrong is deliberately unfixed — Craig's choice, made at the desk, not here.canon
  • The real poem: Henry Kendall's "Bell-Birds" (1869), from Leaves from Australian Forests — a school-anthology staple memorised by generations. Its own final stanza is a preservation project: keeping "in the city and alleys / The beauty and strength of the deep mountain valleys." The poem was already trying to be a node. Nobody in the scene may notice this.canon
  • Erring together (payoff, not payload): Katita and the tree sharing the altered poem is later one of the dramatisations of truth staying alive through contested relation. That meaning belongs to the aftermath; here it is only two voices agreeing.canon

Dialogue permissions

Katita may

Recite — more than once, unchanged, the wrong word kept. Answer a correction with a shrug, a repetition, or by continuing the stanza. Say something small and practical ("That's how I have it"). Fall asleep. Her calm must read as bodily, not oracular — she is tired, warm, sure of her own mouth, not delivering a philosophy of emergence.

Leif may

Quote the stored line aloud once, flatly, as evidence. Ask where she learned it (school, a grandmother, a browning anthology — the answer can stay vague). Narrate his search physically: scrolling, checking a second node's copy, hunting the truck for a printed book that isn't there. Savour, briefly and privately, the correction he thinks he's about to make.

No one may

Say provenance, memory, archive, corruption, version as concepts. List the four possibilities aloud — they may exist only as the shape of Leif's silent checking, and even his narration should run out before it numbers them. Argue. Reach a conclusion. Treat the moment as important while it is happening. The scene's entire meaning must be carried by what hands and eyes do next.

Sensory bank — raw material, take what serves

  • Dusk camp by a creek; actual bellbirds still chiming in the dense green upstream — single struck notes, no melody, a colony sounding like slow rain on a tin roof. The poem's subject audibly present and entirely uninterested.
  • The recitation over some task: washing up in a basin, coiling cable, driving the last unsealed kilometres with the window down — verse fitted to work rhythm, the road-years habit.
  • The node's small LCD in the failing light; the file arriving at 300 bits per second — the poem loading slower than she speaks it, line assembling under line, so his correction has to wait for the text to finish being true.
  • Laptop glow against bark; moths hitting the screen and leaving powder; the keyboard gritty with campsite dust.
  • No signal out here in any direction that isn't theirs — nothing to check the canonical text against except their own archive, which is unanimous.
  • The kinds of places a printed copy would live: a browned school anthology, an op-shop Selected Australian Verse with a name in pencil inside the cover, a country-library stack two towns back. All of them elsewhere tonight.
  • Smells: creek mud, crushed eucalyptus leaf on the air, two-stroke on his hands from the generator, tea going cold on the tailgate.
  • Night sounds: the creek working, a mopoke far off, the tink of the last bellbirds thinning as the light goes, canvas ticking as the dew arrives.
  • Bell miners in fact: colony birds that hold a stretch of creek, drive other species out, and farm the insects that sicken the very trees they sing from — the loveliest sound in the valley implicated in the canopy's slow decline. One touch, if any; zero comment.
  • Her mouth in torchlight, unhesitating on the wrong word; his thumb holding his place on a scroll wheel; the physical grammar of checking versus reciting.

Traps — the known failure modes

  • Explaining the hinge. The moment anyone (including the narration) lays out the four causal possibilities as a list, the scene dies. The collapse must be enacted — checks that fail, one after another, physically — not summarised.
  • Fixing the word. The scaffold must not choose Katita's alteration and neither may a clever draft sentence that quotes "her version" in full. Write around the word or leave the slot visibly Craig's. (The lives/leaves drift already exists elsewhere in the archive as canon — reusing it here versus keeping her word separate is a real decision; make it deliberately.)
  • The argument. If Leif and Katita debate what it means, the split has been spent for the price of a fleck. Their divergence tonight is postural — one reads, one recites — and neither names it.
  • Making Katita mystic. Her lack of disturbance is not serenity or secret knowledge. She is a tired woman who trusts her own memory and doesn't need the file to agree with her. Keep her hands full and her answers short.
  • Quaintness. Kendall must not arrive as heritage furniture. The poem is a working object — memorised, useful, worn smooth like the taped paperbacks. No lyre-bird reverence.
  • Sleek horror. The convergence must feel botanical, humid, slow — an agreement discovered by torchlight — never an interface event. No screen may do anything a 1980s screen couldn't.

Ten sparks — optional ways in, steal freely

  1. Open on the real bellbirds — the colony chiming upstream while she recites the poem about them, so the valley is briefly saying the same thing twice, in two codecs, neither canonical.
  2. Leif catches the wrong word instantly and says nothing — holding the correction in his mouth like a boiled lolly, planning to produce the file with a flourish. The pettiness humanises him; the file's betrayal of it is the scene's first cold touch.
  3. The stored poem loads at 300 bits per second: line under line, the altered line assembling letter by letter while he waits to be right. Give the reader the wait, not the word.
  4. Candidate types of alteration to write around: a softened verb; a creek moved from one stanza's gully to another's; a season shifted by a month; a colour swapped for its neighbour; a plural made singular. Small enough that only love would notice — the tree edits like someone who has heard her say it.
  5. He queries the neighbouring nodes' copies over the mesh, one by one, torch in his teeth. Each agrees. The archive is unanimous the way a family closes ranks.
  6. Her provenance, offered without being asked: Year Five, a teacher who made them stand; or her grandmother, who had it off a teacher who made her stand. Two bodies deep before any file. Leave it unverifiable.
  7. A browned anthology remembered but not present — Leaves from Australian Forests spotted once in an op-shop two towns back and not bought. He thinks of driving back for it. It's three hours. He doesn't. (Leaf / Leif hums under this; nobody articulates it.)
  8. Asked to do it again, she recites the stanza a second time, identical, wrong word kept — not defiance, just her memory holding its line. Whether she is siding with her own mouth or with the tree is unaskable, and he doesn't ask.
  9. One touch of true ecology: the bellbird colony means the canopy above it is being slowly farmed sick — the song and the damage one system. Let Leif know this the way rural people know things, in passing, and let it colour nothing openly.
  10. End physical: she's asleep in the swag mid-stanza-four; he sits with the laptop lid lowered to half-mast like not staring at someone; the last bellbird stops; the creek keeps transmitting.

Exit condition

Three pages. The scene ends with nothing established — no cause chosen, no argument had, no word revealed to the reader as definitively hers or the tree's first. Leif's checking has run out of places to check; Katita is asleep or nearly; the birds have handed the valley to the creek. The reader should close the scene having watched a small domestic moment — a woman reciting, a man looking something up — and only later feel that this was the night the archive and the woman became impossible to tell apart. If the hinge explains itself anywhere, the scene has failed; if the reader can adjudicate the four causes, it has failed twice.
