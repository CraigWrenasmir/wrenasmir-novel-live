SCENE SCAFFOLD

The Storm Crossing the Mesh

Rank: The Road Years Alive Tier: FULL SCENE · 3 pp Plan §10 Band: Root One of the six ratified full scenes

The job — what this scene must accomplish

This is the crucial establishing event of the whole corruption arc, and it must establish it as wonder, not warning. Full-scene treatment: room, bodies, weather, silence, consequences. The loads:

  • The network becomes a body — for the reader. A storm crosses the valley and the mesh reports it in sequence: the far node feels the front before the truck does. For the first time the network is one body long enough to experience weather moving through it. Leif and Katita must not arrive at this thought on the page; the staging arrives at it for them.
  • The same weather on their skin. The scene's engine is the gap between report and arrival — the mesh telling them their own next twenty minutes, and then the rain making good on it against the cab. Screen and skin must alternate until they close on each other.
  • One node goes dark and comes back. A small death and a return inside the big weather. Play it as maintenance jeopardy — a battery, a connector — and let the return be relief nobody dwells on. The corruption arc starts here precisely because nothing corrupt happens: the reader simply learns the mesh can be felt for.
  • Fund the marriage. Two bodies in a cab for three pages, easy with each other. This is the truck at its warmest — the same cab the hammer walk leaves from and the split divides. Every degree of warmth banked here pays interest there.

Where it sits

Currently remainder pp. 15–17 (≈ MS pp. 67–69) — the summit of "The Road Years Alive," after the heritage tour, with the cicada fleck (p. 18) ratified as its comic counterweight immediately following. The order can shift on the Pressure Map, but the cicadas should stay adjacent wherever it lands: the deflation is part of the design.

Feeds directly into: organic integration and the whole co-authorship band — mould, ants, resin, file drift all become legible as this body's slow life; the amber fleck (lightning will one day stop counting seconds and hit); Bell-Birds; the hammer; the split. Pays off from: the rooftop napkin — enough routes that the message survives losing one — enacted here as weather, when the mesh routes around the dark node. The payoff must not be remarked; it is for the reader who kept the napkin.

Dialogue permissions

Katita may

Read timestamps aloud, low, like someone keeping score of a game only she is playing. Count down the front's arrival from the far node's report and be wrong by ninety seconds. Put a flat hand on the dash and wait. Be the first to stop watching the screen and just listen. Say the dark node's name once, the way you say the name of a dog that's out in it.

Leif may

Worry in pure maintenance vocabulary — battery, connector, water in the housing, whether he seated the bark plate properly last visit. Refresh too often. Do the arithmetic of lightning by seconds and say only the number. Go completely quiet when the node comes back, and top up the thermos lid as if that were the response.

No one may

Say body, alive, feels, nervous, breathing, one thing — no anatomy, no animism, no summary. Name the event or toast it. Connect the mesh's routing to the napkin, the night to the project, or the storm to anything. The biggest sentence spoken aloud in the scene should be about something small: the tea, the wipers, whether the window seal will hold.

Sensory bank — raw material, take what serves

  • Before: the green-black anvil standing off the range at four o'clock; the paddock noise stopping all at once — birds first, then insects — the land going quiet in the order of who can hear it coming.
  • Lightning on the AM band before it's visible: the radio crackling under a request show, each crack a strike somewhere over the horizon — the oldest node on the mesh, and nobody's.
  • The LCD's slow arithmetic: humidity climbing by whole digits; the pressure line stepping down like a staircase drawn at 300 bits/sec; reports arriving from trees they can name.
  • The gust front: grass going silver side up in a travelling line, the windmill spinning up and then braking itself, the truck rocking once on its springs like a boat taking a wake.
  • First drops in dust — coins of mud on the windscreen, countable, then not countable. The dirt smell rising off the paddock as if the rain were unwrapping it.
  • The roof: individual drops, then the drum, then — if wanted — hail's different voice for one minute, a harder instrument the cab is inside of. Speech pointless; the thermos lid passed instead.
  • Windows fogging; a porthole wiped with a forearm; the wet-wool-and-tea smell of two people in a sealed cab; her feet in his lap or his coat across her knees — decide by temperature, not by tenderness quota.
  • The dark node: a report that stops mid-line on the screen and stays stopped. The gap where its next timestamp should be, refreshing into the same gap.
  • Strike-count: flash to thunder, four seconds, then two, then a strike near enough to arrive in the chest cavity — and, two seconds ahead of their ears, a pressure spike already logged from the ridge. Do not remark it.
  • The return: the dark node coming back with its backlog in one burst — the screen filling with everything it went on saying while nobody could hear it. It kept logging in the dark.
  • After: drips on the ten-second interval; frogs starting up as if switched; the creek audible where it wasn't before; steam off the bonnet; the whole valley rinsed and ticking.
  • Consequences, next morning: one bark plate to re-tie; the resin smell of wet eucalypt; ants already back at the warm housing; the day's job list unchanged.

Traps — the known failure modes

  • The thesis. One line of "it's like it's —" from either of them and the scene is dead. The recognition lives entirely in staging: the screen saying rain, then the rain. If a draft needs the thought stated, state it in the draft and cut it in the pass.
  • Disaster pacing. Nothing is at stake for the hardware except one node's evening. If the storm reads as threat — to the truck, the project, the people — the scene has borrowed the wrong genre. The jeopardy budget is a battery.
  • Omen-lighting the dark node. It comes back. If the prose lingers in the gap with foreboding, the corruption arc is spoiled at birth. The gap is worry sized for a connector, and the return closes it without ceremony.
  • Log outrunning body. Three pages of two people in a cab: the screen must never hold the camera for more than a few lines before skin takes it back. Alternate registers until they converge; the converging is the scene.
  • Poeticising the log. Log lines stay dry — numbers, timestamps, node names. The prose around them carries all feeling. A lyrical log line here bankrupts Bell-Birds, where the file's wrongness must land as impossible.
  • Summary-time. The storm crosses in scene-time, minute by minute. No "all night the storm..." until the final half-page is ready to dissolve to morning — and morning is maintenance, not meaning.

Ten sparks — optional ways in, steal freely

  1. Open on the radio hearing it first: lightning as static punctuation under a country request show, a dedication going out to a couple celebrating fifty years, interrupted by strikes neither the announcer nor the couple can hear yet.
  2. The far node reports rain at 9:41. Katita puts her hand flat on the dash and they sit inside the strange interval of knowing their own weather twelve minutes early — the future arriving at 300 bits/sec, slower than the wind that carries it.
  3. The countdown game: she calls the front's arrival from the timestamps, sets the thermos lid down as a stopwatch, and is wrong by ninety seconds — and her being delighted by the error, not corrected by it, is the whole of her later philosophy in one beat. No comment.
  4. The node goes dark mid-report — the line ends mid-word and the screen holds the half-word on its little grey face. Leif lists causes aloud like a man naming his worries to shrink them: battery, connector, water. Katita says the node's name once and turns the radio down, as if that would help it.
  5. Hail for one minute: the cab too loud for speech, the conversation continued in glances, the tea passed at the right moment anyway — the private bodily language doing its work under pressure. One touch of the primes rhythm tapped on her knee, unconscious. One touch.
  6. The close strike: chest-thump, both of them ducking on reflex and laughing at themselves — then both leaning to the screen without a word to see if the ridge felt it too. It did, two seconds early. Neither says so. The scene's centre of gravity, played as a glance.
  7. The return: the screen floods with the dark node's backlog — twenty minutes of faithful reporting into nothing, delivered all at once — and Leif reads it the way you'd listen to someone tell you where they'd been. He says only: "seated the plate wrong." Maintenance vocabulary carrying everything.
  8. Katita falls asleep before the storm has fully crossed; Leif watches the tail of the front move node to node off the eastern edge of the mesh alone, and turns the screen brightness down like a man closing a door on a sleeping house.
  9. Afterwards they get out into the dripping dark, bare feet in wet grass, and stand where the report said the storm would be, inside the fact of it having been right. Frogs. The creek. Nobody says anything at all for a paragraph the reader can hear.
  10. End on consequences at first light: the re-tied bark plate, the resin smell, ants back at the warm housing, the day's list unchanged — the morning written so ordinarily that the size of the night is invisible to everyone who was inside it.

Exit condition

Three pages. The scene ends in ordinary morning maintenance with nothing said and nothing decided. The reader — and only the reader — leaves knowing the network was one body long enough to feel a storm move through it; if Leif or Katita visibly know it too, cut the lines where they show it. The night should read as one of the best of their lives without anyone inside it ranking it. Every later horror of the corruption must be able to point back here and find only wonder, weather, and two people warm in a cab — that innocence is the scene's cargo, and it travels unmarked.
