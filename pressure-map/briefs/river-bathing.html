SCENE SCAFFOLD

River Bathing

Rank: Origins & Pleasure Tier: FULL SCENE · 2 pp Band: Root Plan §8: the road years One of the six ratified full scenes

The job — what this scene must accomplish

This is THE happy scene — the one genuinely happy road-years full scene the ranking demands, and its job is brutally simple: the fracture must be emotionally funded, not just intellectually legible. Twenty-odd pages from now the hammer and the split have to hurt, and this is the deposit they draw against. Three loads:

  • Skin, water, light, laughter — zero philosophy. Two people washing in a brown river at dusk, cattle watching, the current doing the rinsing. Nothing else is asked of the page and nothing else is permitted on it.
  • Full scene-time. This is one of the six blocks that get room, bodies, weather, silence and consequences. Minutes pass at the pace of minutes. Let there be a long stretch where nothing happens except water.
  • The road years become actual life. Not a montage of quirk, not rural Australia as a grid for infrastructure — a couple who enjoy inhabiting it, bad food and excellent light, and this evening the light is very good.

Ratified guardrail: the reader must feel what is lost before it is lost — and the scene itself must never know it is that.

Where it sits

Currently remainder pp. 6–7 (≈ MS pp. 58–59) — the crown of the Origins & Pleasure band, after the digital worlds and before the Phone Box fleck closes the band. The order can shift on the Pressure Map; the ranking also allows war-dialling to carry the happy full scene instead — as currently laid, this page carries it.

Feeds directly into: the whole Road band — this is the register the road years are made of — and, at long range, the hammer and the split, which spend what this scene banks. Pays off from: nothing it needs to acknowledge. The truck, the road, the modules exist offstage; the scene arrives mid-life, mid-trip, mid-dusk.

Dialogue permissions

Katita may

Laugh, and be the cause of laughter. Issue practical instructions (hold this, mind the snag, don't drop the soap). Address the cattle directly and give one of them a name. Wash his hair badly, soap in his eyes, and accept no criticism. Be silent for a long stretch without the silence meaning anything.

Leif may

Fuss — footing, leeches, the whereabouts of the soap — and be laughed out of it. Go under and come up facing the wrong way and like it. Watch her with his whole attention and say nothing about it. Make one bad joke that works. Be hungry.

No one may

Mention the modules, the network, the mission, or the future in any tense. Have an insight. Say anything about rivers, time, memory or change — the entire Heraclitus neighbourhood is embargoed. Notice happiness as happiness ("I'm so happy" and all its cousins are forbidden). Say anything a reader could underline as theme. The scene's vocabulary is soap, mud, cow, dark, dinner.

Sensory bank — raw material, take what serves

  • The water: opaque brown, blood-warm at the surface with cold stripes at the knees; the current a slow steady hand; footing of silt, sunken branches, one sudden hole.
  • The light: the west going the colour of the river; the water holding the sky better than the sky holds it; river red gums going black against it.
  • The soap: one bar, gone gluey in its dish; lather taken downstream the instant it forms; the ceremonial trudge back up the bank because somebody left it on the rock again.
  • The cattle: come down to drink and stay to watch; absolute absence of opinion; one cow coughing like a door; hooves sucking in the mud when they shift.
  • Sounds: corellas going over in one long argument; the river's small talk against a snag; something turning over once midstream; no highway anywhere.
  • Skin: sunburn lines glowing in the cool water; days of dust lifting off in a faint halo; hair slicked to skulls making them both look younger and slightly criminal.
  • The bank: clothes on a branch, one towel between them, thongs abandoned at angles, dung dodged barefoot, the mosquitoes arriving on schedule at last light.
  • The truck up the slope with both doors open, ticking as it cools; the plastic tub where the clean shirts live, smelling of the tub.
  • After: wet footprints drying on dust, the tailgate as a bench, damp seats, driving the last hour with wet hair and the windows down, insects in the headlights like static.

Traps — the known failure modes

  • The elegy leak. The deadliest one. No future tense, no "they would remember this," no shadow at the edge of the frame. The moment the prose knows this is THE happy scene, it curdles into foreboding and the funding fails. The scene must be stupid with the present.
  • Philosophy in the water. Zero, including smuggled. If either character observes anything about rivers, time or bodies, cut the sentence. The bushwalk and the bedroom own ideas; this page owns soap.
  • The postcard. Dusk-purple prose, golden light by the tin-full. The scene earns its lyricism through accuracy: the water stays brown, the mud real, the cow coughs. One beautiful sentence per page maximum, and it should be about something plain.
  • Rapture over laughter. It's erotic, but the job is intimacy-as-ordinariness. If it becomes a love scene the ordinary evening is lost, and the ordinary evening is the asset.
  • Certifying the year. Nothing may pin where in the road years this sits — no module count, no anniversary, no "by then." Chronology stays backstage.
  • Ending on a freeze-frame. Full scenes carry consequences. Don't end in the water; end in motion — hunger, the dark drive, the next small thing. The evening continues past the page.

Ten sparks — optional ways in, steal freely

  1. Open with the soap, not the river: one bar gone gluey in a dish since three towns back, and the argument — conducted entirely in eyebrows — about whose turn it is to carry it down the bank.
  2. The cattle arrive one at a time, like an audience that heard late. Katita curtsies, naked, formally. The cattle continue chewing. Nothing else in the scene needs to be funnier than this.
  3. The current game: lie flat, let it carry you down to the snag, wade back, again. And again. No one wins, nothing is learned, the light goes while they're still playing.
  4. She washes his hair with great confidence and no skill; soap in his eyes twice; he trusts her less each time and keeps submitting. Wordless.
  5. A sock, lost to the river, farewelled with full military honours from the bank.
  6. The cold stripe: a spring feeding in somewhere below. They keep trying to stand exactly in it and can't find it twice. Neither says a word about it — not one.
  7. The mosquito bargain: the precise minute the river stops being worth it, announced by Katita slapping her own shoulder mid-sentence and walking out of the water without finishing the sentence.
  8. The clean-shirt ceremony: last clean shirts each, saved for after washing, pulled from the plastic tub and smelling of the tub — the road-years version of dressing for dinner.
  9. Leif goes under, comes up facing the wrong way, spits brown, grins. The river has turned him around and he liked it. Do not gloss it.
  10. End in motion and appetite: her driving barefoot and wet, insects in the headlights, somebody's stomach growling loud enough to be answered — end mid-laugh, or on the word for whatever the next town might feed them.

Exit condition

Two pages at full scene-time. It ends on appetite or laughter, in motion, nothing decided and nothing noticed. No line of it should be quotable as meaning anything beyond its evening. Its success is measured twenty-five pages away: the hammer and the split must hurt because of this page. If the scene feels significant while it's happening, the funding has leaked; if it feels like the best ordinary evening of somebody else's life, it's done.
