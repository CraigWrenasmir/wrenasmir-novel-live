SCENE SCAFFOLD

Heritage Tour of the Dead Server

Rank: The Road Years Alive Tier: Medium · 2 pp Plan §7 · staged here Band: Vaporwave The avatars stay unresolved

The job — what this scene must accomplish

Two pages of shared digital pleasure with one cold current through it. The loads:

  • Play before mission, again. This is the §7 world — obsolete platforms, low-poly Newcastle, hardware tiny and light enormous — staged during the road years as digital play at night. The tour is a game they run for fun. ROOT's ancestry in pleasure, not collapse-fear, gets its clearest single demonstration here.
  • Leif as tour guide. He conducts an actual local-history tour through the abandoned render — patter, dates, "on your left" — his obsessions given a civic, generous shape. The reader sees what his aftermath decade of "research" will be built from, without being told.
  • Three silent avatars begin following him. This is the scene's cold current and its whole afterlife. They arrive, they follow, they never speak, and who they are is never resolved — not in this scene, not in the book. The mystery must be handled so lightly that a first reader could take them for set dressing and a second reader can't sleep about them.

Coordination (2026-07-24): Play Before Mission now stages the OTHER sim — an overgrown far-future Newcastle, vines and reeds, explored as a couple's curiosity. This tour's world must stay distinct: period-accurate, as-it-was, dead-server Newcastle — no vegetation reclaiming anything here, no future register. That sim looks forward; this one looks back. The pairing is the point, and neither scene may acknowledge the other.

Where it sits

Currently remainder pp. 13–14 (≈ MS pp. 65–66) — after the Big Thing, before the storm. The placement makes a quiet triptych of obscured civic objects: monument, render, mesh. The order can shift on the Pressure Map; the plan allows this block to be staged here or revisited later, so it also survives transplanting into the aftermath as memory.

Feeds directly into: the storm (pp. 15–17 — from a dead virtual world to a live physical one overnight); much later, the diagnostic render of the Wound — the misrendered harbour and low-poly light return as ROOT's own interface, and this scene is why that interface will feel like a homecoming. Pays off from: Play Before Mission (the origins block), and the old maps fleck — the render is one more outdated inscription he prefers to the updated world.

Dialogue permissions

Leif may

Do full tour-guide voice — dates, provenance, "if you'll follow me." Deliver the history of a demolished building in the present tense because here it is present. Get one date slightly wrong, uncorrected. Address the avatars as a tour group with complete courtesy and no questions. Enjoy himself without reservation.

Katita may

Drive her avatar badly and not care. Sit in geometry that isn't solid. Heckle the guide the way locals heckle a tour about their own town. Notice the followers first and say only "you've got customers." Log off before him, or fall asleep in the bunk while he finishes.

No one may

Speak as the avatars — they type nothing, emote nothing, and never resolve. Speculate aloud about who or what they are; no candidate may be floated even to be dismissed. Say haunted, ghosts, watching, witnesses. Turn the tour investigative — nobody inspects a name tag up close, checks a profile, or follows a follower.

Sensory bank — raw material, take what serves

  • The frame: the truck at night, laptop on the fold-down, inverter whine, moths on the screen — the enormous virtual light pouring out of hardware you could hold in two hands.
  • Old light: the server sun stuck at late afternoon forever, shadows baked into the ground textures at an hour that never changes; everything gilded and slightly wrong.
  • Draw distance: fog eating the harbour at three hundred metres; the far crane assembling itself out of nothing as you approach, faithful as a butler.
  • The water: a tiled texture sliding at one speed, seams visible on the diagonal; the tide a setting somebody chose once and never revisited.
  • Vanished buildings standing: what the city lost to the earthquake and the demolition crews still rendered here — a pub that fell, a tower that came down, tram sheds — the server never told they were gone.
  • Low-poly silos: six-sided cylinders, untextured on the harbour face where nobody was expected to look. The wharf planked with one plank, repeated.
  • Billboards advertising businesses dead twice over — once in the world, once when the last player left.
  • Platform physics: name tags rendering before bodies; footsteps out of sync with feet; walking through a wall into a shop interior that is one room deep and full of the same chair.
  • Teleport pads to deleted parcels: THIS LAND IS ABANDONED, in the platform's cheerful default font.
  • The avatars: default skins or near-default, a beat behind, keeping formation the way things keep formation when nobody has told them to stop. They arrive separately. They leave — or don't — unobserved.
  • Sound: the platform's ambient loop, forty seconds of gulls and harbour wash, audibly a loop; under it the real night outside the truck, which has better birds.

Traps — the known failure modes

  • Solving the avatars. Any clue is a solution on layaway — a familiar gait, a suggestive name tag, a meaningful log-off. Give the reader nothing that could ever be cashed. The scene's discipline is that Leif doesn't investigate either.
  • Horror drift. The followers are not menace. The scene is pleasure with a cold current, not dread with jokes. If the prose's pulse rises when they appear, rewrite until the tour simply continues.
  • The nostalgia essay. No paragraph about dead platforms, old internets, or what we lost. The render is loved the way the taped paperback is loved — in use, specifically, without a eulogy.
  • Leaking the diagnostic. No ASCII, no status lines, no system text beyond the platform's own dumb signage. That grammar belongs to the corruption, later; borrowing it here spends it and collapses the ontology early.
  • The lecture, again. Tour patter is Leif's rooftop failure mode in a hat. Let lag, a crash, Katita's heckling, or the arrival of a follower interrupt him at the worst moments; the tour proves itself alive by surviving them.

Ten sparks — optional ways in, steal freely

  1. Open mid-patter, no establishment: "On your left, the wharf, which is still here —" and let the reader work out which side of the screen they're on. The truck resolves around the laptop a paragraph later.
  2. The tour distinguishes, without flagging it, between what the render kept and what the city kept: he walks the group through the doors of a building that fell in the earthquake and gives its history in the present tense, because here the present tense is simply accurate.
  3. The first follower arrives during the silo stretch — a name tag rendering out of the fog before its body does, so that for four seconds the tour is being followed by a name.
  4. They arrive one at a time over twenty minutes, spaced like an audience assembling for a country hall meeting; by the wharf there are three, and the prose never counts them again.
  5. Katita's avatar sits on a bollard that isn't solid and sinks to mid-thigh; she stays like that for the rest of the tour, perfectly at ease inside the error. One image of her whole philosophy; no comment.
  6. The tide is wrong: he knows the real harbour's tide tonight — knows it the way he knows the payphone number — and the render's water is doing something else. He notices, says nothing, and the tour moves on. This is what noticing is for.
  7. Lag: mid-sentence the followers walk through him and reassemble behind, the way a wave passes through weed. The platform's fault, entirely explicable, and nobody explains it.
  8. He times the tour to end "at dusk" out of professional habit, though the server sun hasn't moved in nine years. He wraps up facing the permanent afternoon: "and that concludes —" to three default skins and a tiled sea.
  9. Katita is asleep in the bunk by the end; the last third of the tour is delivered in a whisper so as not to wake her — a heritage tour at whisper volume, for three strangers, in a city that no longer exists twice.
  10. Log-off order as the exit: he leaves first, deliberately, tipping an invisible hat — leaving them standing on the wharf. What the render does when no one is watching is not shown, not wondered about, not written.

Exit condition

Two pages. The scene ends in the truck's real dark — laptop lid, inverter off, actual birds — with the tour having been, on its face, nothing but a good night. Nobody has wondered aloud. The three avatars have not been explained, approached, or even counted twice, and the reader should only notice on reflection that the scene declined every chance to look closer. If the followers feel like a plot thread, tighten until they feel like weather. The mystery is not a debt the book owes; it is a possession the book keeps.
