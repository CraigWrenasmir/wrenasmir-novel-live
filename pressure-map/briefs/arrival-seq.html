SCENE SCAFFOLD

The Arrival

Rank: Return & Arrival Tier: FULL SCENE · 4 pp Remainder pp. 46–49 ≈ MS pp. 98–101 Plan §§21 · 25–28 · Band: Train Condition: ATTENTION

The job — what this scene must accomplish

This is the end of the book's prose. One of the six ratified full scenes, and the fourth warning's home ground: only the final-cluster elements are active — the ostinato in the slowing train, the small tree and wren at the station, the uncertain human arrival, and then the silent ASCII map that follows on its own page. Everything else the book has ever carried stays subterranean, already vibrating underneath. Four loads:

  • Resolve the phrase in the body. The train's slowing rhythm aligns with Leif's unconscious tapping and resolves it by one added beat. Page 1's ostinato, her prime game, and a decade of motel-table drumming close in a single physical event — felt in the hand, never named as arithmetic.
  • Deliver the Adlestrop minute. A young tree growing in ballast or some other inhospitable margin, the whole tree moving in the wind, perhaps a wren entering or leaving. Little happens; the world becomes briefly receivable. No explicit revelation.
  • Keep the arrival uncertain without bleaching it. Signs of the friend — all the emotional alternatives simultaneously available, none certified. The ambiguity means Leif can no longer inhabit presence without residual uncertainty about authorship, projection and reception — it does not mean nothing is real. The relief is real. The book does not adjudicate.
  • End in motion. Alighting, breath, ballast, wind, a gate or first step, perhaps a human figure, perhaps the space where one may appear. The last image of the book's prose is the beginning of the walk. The closest thing to revelation is attention itself — as compass, never as sentence.

Underneath, unresolved and unargued: the double exposure. Human relief and the trees as possible witnesses — the reunion's warmth carrying one degree of strangeness it never explains.

Canon — locked in the Bible

  • Scene-world: the present-tense train, Newcastle toward Lake Macquarie, morning; Leif is 42, "on my way to see my friend"; the empty platform at Fassifern has already passed the window earlier in the book. The final station itself is unnamed in canon — small, regional, on the lake line.canon
  • The ostinato is page 1: pylons like bar lines, phasing bass notes against the carriage floor, "a known wobbly offbeat." The tapping is simultaneously Katita's prime game, bodily memory, a protocol, and a musical phrase awaiting resolution; the slowing train aligns with it and resolves by one added beat.canon
  • The friend is never named in the prose. The narrator ratified it on the page: "from herein, I'll always refer to as my friend." Andrew exists backstage only. They meet every six months and embrace "as brothers who survived the teenage years, the courting years, the apprentice years."canon
  • Meeting logistics: the friend collects him; "we will park outside the bush as always and then my friend will drive us in his capable four-wheel drive across the first ridge of the mountain that necklaces the region."canon
  • The menu of uncertain signs (§27): a sign he has been there · a vehicle absent or partly seen · a boot mark · a message · a figure near the trees · the suggestion he is around the corner · a glimpse that may or may not resolve. The alternatives remain simultaneously available: he is physically present; he is perceived through memory and expectation; he has been and may return; the walk will occur; the walk is already occurring inside Leif's long rehearsal of it. The book need not adjudicate.canon
  • The small tree (§26): growing in ballast, concrete, fencing or some other inhospitable margin; the whole tree moves in the wind; perhaps a wren enters or leaves; the dead node's nest is recalled without explanation; Adlestrop register — little happens, and the world becomes briefly receivable.canon
  • The double exposure (✦ The Wound): the relief of the friend is real, and the trees around the meeting are no longer innocent scenery — possible witnesses, listening structurally. Keep both; resolve neither.canon
  • The older ending image survives as one option: Leif sees the friend before the friend sees him — adjusting something, tying a shoe, looking away. Understated, slightly off-centre, human. It is now one alternative on the §27 menu, not the certified outcome.canon
  • Edward Thomas is already inside the book: "Edward Thomas for the Space Age" — the Adlestrop register arrives as a return, not an import.canon
  • States: Leif carried, not steering — movement without control. Recognition, not resolution: a person may not be knowable as an answer, but they can still be met. Philosophical condition: Attention — the closest thing to revelation.canon

Dialogue permissions

Leif (narration) may

Feel the added beat as a physical event and look at his own hand. Leave the station unnamed and know it by the length of its platform. Recall the wren nest in a single clause — warmth arriving in his fingers — and let it go in the same sentence. Register the trees' attention as a temperature, a pressure, a politeness, never as a claim. Breathe, and notice breathing.

The world & the friend may

Supply a garbled announcement whose words never resolve. Provide crossing bells, the brake's long exhale, a door chime, wind. Offer at most two signs from the §27 menu. The friend may appear or not appear; if he appears, he may be mid-ordinary-task — adjusting a strap, tying a lace, looking away — may raise a hand, and may say nothing, or one plain word half-taken by wind.

No one may

Summarise the philosophy, or utter any sentence that could be quoted as the book's thesis. Explain the beat, the tree, or the trees. Name Katita, ROOT, the Boxer, the courtesan, Elias Shore, or the title phrase — they are pressure here, not names. Say witness, archive, AI, system. Adjudicate the friend's presence in either direction — no confirmation, no debunking, and no coy winking at the uncertainty. Deliver a grand speech, or any speech.

Sensory bank — raw material, take what serves

  • The deceleration changing time signature: the long air-brake exhale, couplings taking up slack carriage by carriage, the ostinato's offbeat stretching as the bar lines (pylons) space out.
  • Level-crossing bells rising and falling past the window; boom-gate counterweights; then no more crossings — the quiet after the last one as its own event.
  • The platform: cracked concrete apron, yellow tactile studs, a galvanised mesh fence bellied by decades of leaners, a WAY OUT sign to a car park of crushed white gravel.
  • Ballast up close for the first time in the book: blue metal, oil-dark near the rail, sun-pale at the verge; the specific chock and give of a boot landing on it.
  • Lake air through the opening doors: brackish, casuarina resin, a distant two-stroke that could be boat or mower and is never settled.
  • The small tree, margin-true: a eucalypt sapling out of the ballast shoulder, or an angophora seedling in a fence-line crack, or a casuarina whip beside a drainage grate — juvenile leaves oversized for the stem, the whole tree moving as one thing in the wind, not branch by branch.
  • Wren traffic: a male's blue flag crossing the line low, tail vertical; the dive into the small tree; the tree accepting it without pausing in its movement.
  • Heat shimmer down the rails southward; rails ticking as they warm; cicadas at mid-morning volume cutting out all at once and restarting — just weather now, no longer a figure.
  • Signs of the friend, concrete and budgeted: the diesel tick of a recently parked engine; a 4WD's snout half-visible past the pines; one boot print holding red ridge-clay in a puddle margin; a folded paper under a wiper that may only be a parking notice; a figure at the tree line with exactly his stillness.
  • The gate: pipe-and-chainmesh, council green, dragging a quarter-circle groove in the gravel — the groove as the record of every previous opening, this opening entering the record.

Traps — the known failure modes

  • The basket. The fourth warning is law here. Any second-tier motif surfacing by name — Katita, ROOT, the Boxer, Shore, the pamphlet, amber, the masthead — turns the ending into a curtain call. They are allowed only as unnameable pressure. If a callback asks to be recognised, cut it.
  • The summary sentence. Any line that could be quoted as wisdom ("truth is...", "attention is...", "what remains is...") is the essay the whole endgame was built to avoid. If a sentence explains, feed it to the wind.
  • Adjudicating the friend. Confirming him kills the residue; debunking him kills the relief; and the opposite trap — coyness, the was-he-ever-real game — kills both. The ambiguity is Leif's changed relation to presence, not a puzzle set for the reader.
  • Nothing-is-real drift. The uncertainty must not bleach the world. The ballast is hard, the wren is a wren, the diesel tick is hot metal, the relief is real. Ambiguity lives in Leif, not in the scenery.
  • The epiphany at the tree. Adlestrop means little happens. No held breath, no welling, no slow motion, no sentence leaning on the reader's sleeve. The tree is not looked at so much as received — one unscheduled minute, then the doors.
  • Counting the beat. If Leif does arithmetic on the page — primes, added units, resolution theory — the protocol becomes a puzzle solved. The hand knows; the prose watches the hand.
  • Ending in the embrace. The embrace is canon as anticipation and may be allowed to be imminent — but the last image is the beginning of the walk. End mid-motion, feet on ground, not in arms.
  • The trees as horror. The double exposure is one degree of temperature under the relief, not a genre shift. If the trees menace, the book has adjudicated after all — in the wrong direction.

Ten sparks — optional ways in, steal freely

  1. Open on the time-signature change: the ostinato's wobbly offbeat lengthening as the train brakes, and Leif's knuckle already on the window frame — having kept time the whole book without him.
  2. The added beat as a physical event: the final brake-lurch lands one knock his hand didn't make, and the phrase closes from outside his body. He looks at his hand. Paragraph ends.
  3. The announcement names the station in vowels only; he doesn't need it — his legs already know the length of this platform. Named by body, not by sign.
  4. The Adlestrop minute with its mechanics intact: an unscheduled pause before the doors release — a held minute in which nothing happens except the tree, the wren in it, a whipbird further up the ridge, and cicadas rising away over the whole catchment — one sentence widening outward like that, and then the doors.
  5. The wren enters the small tree and doesn't come out; Leif waits one beat longer than the waiting needs — the added beat spent a second time, on a bird — then steps down. Brake ready: if it reads as cute, keep the wren and cut the wait.
  6. Budget the signs to two: the diesel tick of a just-parked engine, and one boot print holding ridge-clay from the mountain the friend would have crossed that morning. Everything else the reader supplies, which is the point.
  7. The figure option: someone at the tree line with exactly his stillness, mid-task — strap, lace, looking away — seen before seeing, the Bible's oldest image of the ending. The prose leaves before eye contact resolves it.
  8. The space option: no figure at all — but the gate stands open at the angle of someone having passed through, its groove in the gravel freshly darker. The walk begins into the space where a person may appear.
  9. The recall, spent in one clause: the gate's pipe is sun-hot under his palm and the warmth arrives from two pages back — the heat sink, the eggs — unexplained, gone by the sentence's end.
  10. The last sentence in present-tense motion: gravel, then clay, breath finding a walking rhythm — a new time signature the book does not stay to learn — and the page ends before the track bends. The silent map takes the line from there.

Exit condition

Four pages. The prose ends at the beginning of the walk — mid-motion, breath and ground, the friend neither confirmed nor dissolved, nothing explained about the beat or the tree or the watching. The reader should feel relief with a residue: met, and still unresolved; arrived, and already moving. Test it both ways: if the last page can be quoted as wisdom, it isn't finished; if it can be walked, it is. Then turn the page to the silent map, which is not this scene's to caption — the prose ends with Leif's attention, and the map belongs to the world that continues after him.
