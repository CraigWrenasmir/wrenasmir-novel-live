Play Before Mission — the Overgrown Sim

Rank: Origins & Pleasure Tier: Medium · 2 pp Plan §7 · direction ratified 2026-07-24 Next to typeset: ≈ MS pp. 43–44 Condition: PLAY

The job — what this scene must accomplish

Two pages inside an abandoned, possibly fan-made sim of Newcastle a thousand years on — vines up the civic buildings, grass through the carparks — entered by Leif and Katita as a curiosity, played for delight. Three loads:

  • Establish that these two loved thinking together. The prize of the scene is not the sim; it's the intimacy of shared imagination — two people wandering versions of the world as flirtation. This is the couple's real dowry, and the split spends it. Every degree of delight banked here pays interest there.
  • Install the two registers. She probes the system — why ivy takes one façade and not another, what the renderer chose to preserve, whether the grass is procedural or hand-placed, what's past the mapped edge. He recognises ghosts — "that used to be the bus interchange," "they've kept the wrong building." Structural curiosity flirting with local memory: the rooftop dynamic, now with a world to hold it.
  • Land on the first module as a toy. The block still exits through the ratified door: the first ROOT hardware arrives as a joke or a gift — an ice-cream container that does one useless lovely thing. No mission, no manifesto, no founding. The sim is the atmosphere; the toy is the destination.

Ratified guardrails: ROOT arises from pleasure, play and curiosity, not collapse-fear — and the sim is a beautiful ruined future someone made, maybe badly, maybe lovingly. A fan project. An oddity found on an abandoned server. Never a disaster model, never "the world after civilisation." If a paragraph must be cut, cut anything that smells of purpose.

Page 2 — ratified beat structure (2026-07-24, supersedes the edge-walk plan)

  • Beat 1 — the wrong bird. A bellbird in the sim; intimate argument. He objects on present-tense grounds (gully bird, none in Civic, the student got it wrong); she dissolves with deep time ("in a thousand years they moved in — the sim isn't wrong, it's patient"). Second texture available: it's a MIDI, wrong in timbre — is a wrong-sounding bellbird still a bellbird? The corruption divergence rehearsed as flirtation; nobody wins. This argument now carries the mapped-edge load; the selvage is banked for the road years.
  • Beat 2 — extraction and dare. He extracts the midi (first act of archiving, custodian as fan). She dares him to do something with it (genesis stays frivolous).
  • Beat 3 — the toy. Ice-cream container, dare honoured, build tiny. No sentence may know what the box becomes.
  • Beat 4 — the clock and the sink. Module to the yard; the wall clock's sync pulse is the only other voice in the house, so the bird sings on the hour because the clock tells it to (first protocol: time itself — never remarked). Ice cream eaten at the sink as engineering requirement; exit on the wrong little song through the louvres.

Long-wire discipline: ROOT's first file and its first corruption share a species — but the Bell-Birds convergence must NEVER call back to the midi. Reader-only. Full canon: TMAPO-Bible.md → ✦ The Sim, the Bellbird Founding & the Three Worlds.

Dialogue permissions

Katita may

Interrogate the renderer aloud as a game: "Why does the ivy take that façade and not this one?" · "Is the grass procedural or did someone place it?" · "What did it decide to keep?" Deliver dry affection — "Civic Station — finally improved." Hunt the mapped edge on purpose. Be genuinely delighted, never analytical past the point of pleasure. Steer his hand on the mouse.

Leif may

Recognise ghosts and correct the record: "That wasn't there." · "They've kept the wrong building." · "No one would miss that carpark." · "I used to know someone who lived near here." Be quietly moved and hide it inside jokes. Defend a demolished building the sim also demolished. Mistake hand-placed for real once.

No one may

Say preserve, culture, archive, collapse, future as freight. Treat the sim as prediction or warning. Wonder aloud who made it with anything more than passing curiosity. Found anything, plan anything, or feel the evening's importance. And the module, when it arrives, may not be explained — it does its one useless lovely thing and the page ends before anyone says why it matters.

Sensory bank — raw material, take what serves

The room (mid-2000s discipline — ADSL, CRTs, no smartphones):

  • Two chairs pulled to one machine; passing the mouse as a form of touch; her hand over his to steer.
  • The CRT's static fur on arm hair; the modem's held breath; a tower case warm as a cat.
  • Instant coffee going cold by the keyboard; the room dark except the screen — enormous rendered daylight pouring out of a small hot box.

The sim:

  • Ferns growing through the frozen escalators of the cracked mall; roots lifting the Beaumont Street footpath in neat procedural waves.
  • The Civic Theatre's awning a hanging garden; the Ocean Baths full of reeds, water rendered as a single sheet of green glass that never moves.
  • A rooftop carpark turned meadow, painted lines still visible under the grass — the sim keeping the parking bays like a memory it won't give up.
  • One absurd surviving sign, plastic unfaded — because plastic doesn't age in a render. (One brand only; ration the flecks.)
  • Birdsong that is one four-second file, looping; the same currawong phrase forever.
  • Texture seams where the vine tiles repeat; a sun slightly too large that nobody fixed; pop-in — a fig tree assembling itself out of fog as they approach.
  • The mapped edge: footpath ending in untextured wireframe, then skybox — the world's selvage, walkable if you're brave.

Traps — the known failure modes

  • Symbolism creep. The moment the sim reads as "the world after collapse," the scene dies. It's a fan project — someone's weird loving folly. Keep the maker's amateurishness visible (seams, loops, the oversized sun); amateurishness is the antidote to prophecy.
  • The thesis leak. "The built world repossessed by other forms of life" is scaffold language, never page language. If either of them understands the evening, cut the understanding.
  • Two-sims blur. No silent avatars here, no period accuracy, no tour patter — those belong to the dead server. This sim is future, private, and theirs.
  • Brand overload. One surviving sign is sublime; three is a car ad. The undated-mythic surface dents fast.
  • The founding ceremony. If the module's arrival feels like chapter one of ROOT, the guardrail is breached. It's a toy. The page ends before it becomes anything.
  • Bloat. Two pages. The sim will offer you ten more wonders than fit; the cut ones become texture for the road years or nothing at all. If it wants a third page, it's stealing from River Bathing.

Ten sparks — optional ways in, steal freely

  1. Open inside the render with no explanation — vines on the Civic facade, the too-big sun — and only then pull back to the dark room, two chairs, one mouse: the world first, the hardware after, pixel-pastoral law enacted by the prose itself.
  2. "Civic Station — finally improved." Deadpan, in passing, while she's looking at something else. The book's whole civic tenderness in four words.
  3. She walks them off the mapped edge on purpose: footpath to wireframe to skybox, and she stands on the untextured selvage like a woman on a jetty, entirely comfortable at the end of the authored world. He wants to go back; she wants to see if it loads more. It doesn't. She stays anyway.
  4. The sim preserves a carpark and has deleted the Cooks Hill Bookstore. Leif is genuinely outraged; Katita is delighted by the arbitrariness of what survives. Neither wins the argument; the argument is the flirtation.
  5. They find the maker's one signature: amid procedural lantana, a single hand-placed rose bush, wildly out of place, tended-looking. Someone loved something here specifically. They leave it alone.
  6. The looping currawong: four seconds, forever. Days later, outside in actual Newcastle, Leif hears a real currawong finish the phrase differently and feels obscurely corrected. One touch, no comment.
  7. Pop-in as tenderness: a fig tree assembles from fog as they walk toward it, and Katita slows down to watch the world decide to exist — rendering as a form of greeting, felt, never phrased.
  8. He mistakes one hand-placed tree for a real memory — "that fig was actually there" — and is wrong; it's the renderer's invention, and his certainty embarrasses him for exactly one sentence.
  9. The module's first test file is the sim's looping bird call, copied over as a joke — the ice-cream container in the corner of the room, playing four seconds of fake currawong on repeat while they fall asleep. (Decades later the nodes trade real birds. Reader-only wiring; use only if it stays a joke on the page.)
  10. End on hardware as body: the container warm on the desk, its one LED going in the dark room like something breathing — and outside the window, dawn starting to render the actual street, enormous and free.

Exit condition

Two pages, ending as play ends — nothing decided, nothing founded, someone falling asleep first. The module does its one useless lovely thing and no sentence explains it. The reader should close the page thinking they watched a couple muck about in an old game and build a toy — and only much later understand they watched the future being loved into existence. If the evening feels important while it's happening, dim it until it doesn't.
