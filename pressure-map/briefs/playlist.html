SCENE SCAFFOLD

The Bushranger's Playlist

Rank: Return & Arrival Tier: Fleck · 1 p Remainder p. 44 ≈ MS p. 96 Plan §25 · Band: Vaporwave Condition: ASSEMBLY

The job — what this scene must accomplish

Shortly before the final station, the book's sounds gather — briefly — into one strange organ-like symphony: a public instrument assembled from everything the book has heard. One page. Three loads, carried in a single breath:

  • The title sounds without being said. The organ the whole book has been building plays once, quietly, in a slowing carriage — and is not remarked on. The task was never to explain the organ; it was to make it sound. This is the sounding.
  • The vaporwave layer files its last render. The playlist is the jetpack bushranger's — which means it is ROOT's diagnostic voice surfacing through Leif's attention one final time, contaminating memory rather than being labelled imagination. After this page, the render retires; only the silent ASCII map inherits its game-world logic.
  • The soundstage is cleared for the ending. The symphony must end by subtraction, leaving the ostinato alone and unresolved — one beat short. The arrival pages spend that shortness. If this page resolves anything, it has stolen from the ending.

Ratified guardrail: this is a brief overheard assembly, not a grand finale. The seam where it ends matters more than any crescendo.

This page is where the book's audio archive is heard as one instrument, once.

Dialogue permissions

The page may

Assemble the sounds without sourcing them. Let the bushranger appear as a flicker of render — visor glare, jetpack idle-hum — inside the audio rather than as a scene. Use one small formal shift (a tracklist like a burnt CD's felt-tip index, a file listing) if the prose pressure genuinely spills. Let a real carriage sound and a remembered sound trade places without flagging the exchange.

Leif may

Notice the moment it stops rather than the moment it starts. Mistake the crossing bells for remembered bells and vice versa. Hear the word organ arrive once as plain description of a sound — never as commentary. Keep tapping without knowing it. Reach for nothing.

No one may

Speak. Source a single track ("that was the modem from the paddocks"). Name the playlist as the book's playlist, or the symphony as a summation. Explain the bushranger — as imagination, diagnosis, grief or ghost. Say archive, memory, AI, system, render. Resolve the ostinato — the added beat belongs to the arrival pages and must not be spent here.

Sensory bank — raw material, take what serves

  • The deceleration's own rhythm section: the long air-brake exhale, the under-floor compressor cycling down, couplings taking up slack one carriage at a time.
  • Level-crossing bells doing their Doppler swoop — rising as the train nears, passing, falling — cousin to the wrens' "bell swoop" from the bushwalk pages; don't point at the kinship.
  • Headphone bleed from a sleeping teenager two seats down: tinny synthesiser wash at exactly the fidelity of mall music heard from the next shop.
  • Cicadas through a briefly opened door at a minor platform — January volume, cutting out all at once and restarting.
  • A phone somewhere in the carriage playing talkback at speaker-compression quality: AM's ghost, detuned by codec instead of distance.
  • The pedal note under everything: the pylons' bar-line bass phasing through the carriage floor, the one constant the mix is built on.
  • Rain on the truck's cab roof as remembered texture — a sound the body files under safety — crossfading into rain on this roof that is not falling; dry sky out the window.
  • Alfred Hill's allegro as remembered warmth: strings through a node's small speaker on a river bank at dusk, slightly slowed by cheap playback.
  • A child tapping the window frame down the carriage; Leif's own knuckle answering before he notices it has joined in.
  • One flicker of the diagnostic food court, sound only: the fountain that emits static instead of water — a single second, gone before it can be furniture.

Traps — the known failure modes

  • The grand finale. The named trap. If the symphony swells, builds, or climaxes, cut until it is something half-caught between stations. One page means one page; the assembly is overheard, not staged.
  • The inventory. Ten sounds listed at equal weight is catalogue pace — the exact After-Page-38 disease. Let three or four be audible in the prose; fold the rest in as undertone.
  • The annotated nostalgia tour. Sourcing each sound to its scene converts the chord into a quiz. No provenance. The mix is owned by no one.
  • Adjudicating the render. "He imagined," "the interface," "a memory of the food court" — any label collapses the ratified seam. Vaporwave contaminates; it is never certified.
  • Motif smuggling. This page is a sound-gate, not a basket. No Katita scene, no Shore, no letters, no amber, no masthead. Sounds only — everything else stays subterranean.
  • Spending the resolution. If the phrase completes here, the arrival has nothing left to do with the body. End short. Hold the shortness like a breath not taken.

Ten sparks — optional ways in, steal freely

  1. Open on the real: crossing bells rising toward the train. The real bell pulls the remembered bells, which pull the church's wind-and-bird geometry, which pulls Bach, which pulls Hill's strings — until Leif can no longer say which sounds are in the carriage. One sentence per handover.
  2. The teenager's headphone bleed is the seed crystal: everything else joins that tiny tin orchestra, the whole book arranging itself around the worst possible speaker — a public instrument with no quality control, which is what public means.
  3. The tracklist as form: a brief italic index like felt-tip on a burnt CD, titles misremembered — rain, truck, unknown · handshake (long version) · bells (approach) · bells (church) — in and out in six lines, no commentary.
  4. The bushranger never appears; only his taste does. The mix has choices in it — an edit, a crossfade, a preference — and the fact of curatorship is the haunting. Someone assembled this. Leave "someone" alone.
  5. One frame of render: visor glare on the window's reflection, and the jetpack's idle-hum tuned — he almost notices — to the ostinato's pedal note. Gone before the sentence ends.
  6. Sound-check logic: each sound arrives once, sets its level, steps back — an organist registering stops before a service no one will attend. (Keep the simile or the image, not both.)
  7. The garbled platform announcement absorbed into the mix as its vocal track — the civic voice as lead instrument, words never resolving into a station name.
  8. He reaches for his phone to record it, and the reaching ends it. Attention could hold the thing; capture couldn't. Use with care — this dramatises the book's compass and sits one step from stating it. If it reads as a lesson, cut the reach and keep the ending.
  9. The one impossible edit, unflagged: rain on the truck crossfades into rain on this train's roof — and the window shows dry sky. Contamination as a splice the prose declines to notice.
  10. End by subtraction: the sounds step out one at a time, the modem handshake's rising question last, leaving the ostinato alone — one beat short of something. The page ends inside that shortness.

Exit condition

One page. It ends before the reader can be sure it happened — a paragraph or two, then ordinary carriage sound returns, minus nothing, plus nothing nameable. The ostinato must leave unresolved, the render must leave retired, and no sentence may survive that explains what was assembled or by whom. If it feels like a finale, halve it. If it feels like something half-heard through a wall as the train slows, it's done.
