Some of the paragraphs were ones I later worked on with Katita to compress into text files on the mesh networks we set up across regional plains not dissimilar to those the Boxer traversed with the courtesan he invited on the road with him. In part what I was doing with Katita was fulfilling some ideology that came about through those letters the Boxer and I wrote to one another. At the time, that summer, when I was a layabout couch surfer, reclining everywhere my body could fall into be it fabric or sand, flesh or grass, being or time, I always had a paperback with me, bought with loose change from the Cooks Hill Bookstore. They were always Australian novels and almost all just skirting the war years, books anticipating a generational event for a country that perpetually feels outside of history and meaning, like we could just as well do nothing with our twenty million lives and the dominant narratives of the globe would not register our national declivity. And yet, outside of the front pages of the world's newspapers, we committed a lot of human acts that enlarged our collective imagination. How large, and to what ratio, is be confirmed.
