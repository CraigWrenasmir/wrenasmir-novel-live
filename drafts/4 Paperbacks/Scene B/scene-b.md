Some of the paragraphs were ones I later worked on with Katita to compress into text files on the mesh networks we set up across regional plains not dissimilar to those the Boxer traversed with the courtesan he invited on the road with him. In part what I was doing with Katita was fulfilling some ideology that came about through those letters the Boxer and I wrote to one another. At the time, that summer, when I was a layabout couch surfer, reclining everywhere my body could fall into be it fabric or sand, flesh or grass, being or time, I always had a paperback with me, bought with loose change from the Cooks Hill Bookstore. They were always Australian novels and almost all just skirting the war years, books anticipating a generational event for a country that perpetually feels outside of history and meaning, like we could just as well do nothing with our twenty million lives and the dominant narratives of the globe would not register our national declivity. And yet, outside of the front pages of the world's newspapers, we committed a lot of human acts that enlarged our collective imagination. How large, and to what ratio, is a fair question. A fair question, if only because the weight of imagination feels like it occupies a lower frequency of mass than it once did, and I say this whilst looking out the window and projecting fictions onto the glass like a transparent comic strip panel with a backlit projection of outskirt tundra rushing past as a zoetrope would animate a galloping horse. The trinity of sensible subjectivity has been disrupted. What used to be a balance between the world experienced in real-time and the imagined world and the represented world has significantly tipped into cyberpastoralist data farms now where representations of the world as video have eclipsed any possibility of restoring equilibrium between the remaining angles of real-time life and daydreamed life. The surplus of the seen has taken all possible versions of the lived and imagined world and represented them through recreations that ensure we will never do anything ever again that can't be cross referenced as already done. This is what I told the Boxer, in letters, and what Katita later shrugged at, touching my face with her moisturised palm to confirm I was  still maintaining  adequate physical form.
