A man takes a train from Newcastle to Lake Macquarie to meet his friend for a bushwalk. The book ends when the friend arrives by car to pick the protagonist up to drive deeper into the bush. Everything else is what happens inside the man's mind during the train journey: memory, reverie, daydream, fantasy, projection, and psychic absorption of the times. But, this train journey is not the foundation, see note on nested ontology.

The book lives in the tension between a calm, innocent, everyday moment (a sunny train ride to meet a friend) and an unnamed, looming pressure in the background (war, history, rupture, the world changing). A key reference mood is the poem Adlestrop by Edward Thomas. In Adelstrop, a train pauses in a seemingly ordinary place, but the reader senses a historical threshold approaching. In my book, the narrator senses something on the horizon too, while remaining in a reverie.

It'll be impressionistic and fluid comprised of six interconnected story worlds:

1. The train journey from Newcastle to Lake Macquarie. Male protagonist (42).

2. The Walk / Male friendship. Two friends in the bush (40s). Conversation, interiority, companionship.

3. Solarpunk mesh journey (late 30s).
This is sourced from a story I had planned called Root. A DIY engineer/hacker and an academic partner travel regional Australia installing mesh nodes that preserve cultural fragments after societal collapse.

4. The Boxer (early 30s).
I wrote a character sketch for this section here: https://www.wrenasmir.com/the-scar-in-the-dirt-is-eleven-kilometres-long
A boxer moving through regional Australia, prize fighting in pubs, accompanied by a courtesan he met in the city.

5. Newcastle summer slacker (late 20s).
A man drifting in Newcastle in summer: paperbacks, beach heat, casual relationships, couch-surfing, cultural daydream.

6. Hyper-aesthetic fantasy thread (early-mid 20s).
A vapourwave food court, a jetpack bush ranger, a woman wearing a hologram skirt.

Other point of inspiration: when Adam Curtis said that any story written about the current period should be a ghost story about how AI represents the end of the past, the end of history. This threads with my thoughts about cultural memory and lived experience against the weight of the digital world and its impact on our daydreams. This doesn't seem like an obvious point of inspiration, but it is the hum outside the train, on the horizon. It is the pause at Adlestrop between one station and the next.
